struct RegimeTermMoments{__T_method}

Names the table of the moments of one term of a sum of regime statistics, which the table of a DiagonalTarget holds.

A term $u_{i}^{2} / \hat{v}_{i}$ reads one estimated variance $\hat{v}_{i} = \sigma_{i}^{2} Q_{i}$, and it is divided by the mean's factor $f = \mathbb{E}[Q^{-1}]$, so it carries the noise $a = 1 / (f Q)$, with $\mathbb{E}[a] = 1$. The root and the log of the sum read that noise through their own variable, the one in which the statistic is linear along the direction where every term carries the same noise: $a^{1/2}$ for FirstMomentRegimeAdjusted and $\ln a$ for LogRegimeAdjusted. For each count, regime_bias_factor gives the triple $(f, b, v)$: the mean's factor, the scale $b$ at which the variable of the method has its mean, and the relative variance $v$ of that variable.

Computes the variance of the log of an estimated variance, from the Laplace transform of Q.

The Mellin transform of $Q' = 2 c Q$ expands about zero as $\mathbb{E}[Q'^{-\sigma}] = 1 + \sigma I_{0} + \sigma^{2} (I_{1} + \gamma I_{0}) + O(\sigma^{3})$, so its first two coefficients are the first two moments of $-\ln Q'$. A scale does not move a variance of a log, so the variance of $\ln Q'$ is that of $\ln Q$. At equal weights it agrees with the trigamma function $\psi'(K / 2)$.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} I_{0} &= \int_{0}^{\infty} \left(G(s) - e^{-s}\right) s^{-1}\, \mathrm{d}s\,, \quad I_{1} = \int_{0}^{\infty} \ln s \left(G(s) - e^{-s}\right) s^{-1}\, \mathrm{d}s\,, \\ \mathrm{Var}[\ln Q] &= 2 (I_{1} + \gamma I_{0}) - I_{0}^{2}\,. \end{align}\]

Where $\gamma$ is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

  • G::VecNum: $G(s)$, the Laplace transform of Q on the grid.
  • h::Number: Step of the grid in $x$.

Returns

  • v::Number: $\mathrm{Var}[\ln Q]$.

regime_bias_table(
    method::Union{RegimeAdjustedMethod, RegimeTermMoments},
    decay::Number,
    K::Integer
) -> Any

Computes the bias factor of a regime statistic that reads one estimated variance, for every count of observations from one to K.

An exponentially weighted variance estimate of $K$ observations is $\hat{v} = \sigma^{2} Q$, with $Q = \sum_{j} w_{j} z_{j}^{2}$ on correctly calibrated returns. The inverse of an estimate is too large on average (Jensen's inequality), and each RegimeAdjustedMethod reads its own moment of it, so regime_bias_factor names the factor of each method. The factors are exact, not asymptotic: at equal weights they agree with $K / (K - 2)$, with the ratio of gamma functions and with the digamma form to $10^{-12}$.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} w_{j} &= c\, \lambda^{j}\,, \quad c = \frac{1 - \lambda}{1 - \lambda^{K}}\,, \\ \mathbb{E}[e^{-tQ}] &= G(2 c t)\,, \quad G(s) = \prod_{j=0}^{K-1} (1 + \lambda^{j} s)^{-1/2}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $w_{j}$: Normalised weight of the observation $j$ steps before the newest.
  • $\lambda$: decay.
  • $G$: Laplace transform of $Q$, on the scale $s = 2 c t$.

Algorithm

  1. Lay a grid of step $h = 1/10$ on $x = \ln s \in [-60, 50]$. The integrands are smooth on it and decay at both ends, the slowest as $e^{x/2}$ below, so the trapezoid rule on it agrees with a grid of step $1/200$ on $[-75, 60]$ to $1.1 \times 10^{-12}$ relative from $K = 5$, at half-lives of 2, 10 and 40.
  2. For each $K$, multiply $G$ by $(1 + \lambda^{K - 1} s)^{-1/2}$ on the log scale, so the whole table costs one pass.
  3. Evaluate the factor of the method with regime_bias_factor.

The mean of $Q^{-1}$ is finite from $K = 3$ and its variance from $K = 5$, so an entry at $K \le 4$ is not read.

Arguments

  • method::Union{<:RegimeAdjustedMethod, <:RegimeTermMoments}: Regime adjustment method, which names the moment, or the table of the moments of one term.
  • decay::Number: Decay of the weights.
  • K::Integer: Largest count of observations in the table.

Returns

  • table::AbstractVector: The factor for each count from one to K, or the moments of RegimeTermMoments.

Computes the bias factor of a regime statistic that reads one HAC-adjusted variance estimate, for every count of observations from one to K.

A HAC estimate of $K$ observations is $\hat{v} = \sigma^{2} Q$ with $Q = z^{\top} A z$, a quadratic form in the correctly calibrated returns. $A = c B$ is banded: the weight of each square on its diagonal, and the Bartlett weight $k_{i}$ times the weight of the newer return at lag $i$. Diagonalised, $Q$ has the law of a plain estimate whose weights are the eigenvalues $\mu$ of $A$, so its Laplace transform is $G(s) = \det(I + s B)^{-1/2}$ on the scale $s = 2 c t$, and regime_bias_factor reads it as it reads the transform of regime_bias_table. The exponential weights break the identity that makes the Bartlett estimate a sum of squares, so $A$ has negative eigenvalues from a count of 5 to 50, by the half-life, and $Q$ can fall below zero. There the moment of $Q^{-1}$ is not finite, and the table reads the moment of the positive part: past the minimum of $G$ the negative eigenvalues rule the transform, so the integral stops there.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} B_{jj} &= \lambda^{j}\,, \quad B_{j, j+i} = B_{j+i, j} = \lambda^{j} k_{i}\,, \quad k_{i} = 1 - \frac{i}{L + 1}\,, \quad i = 1, \ldots, L\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $j$: Index of an observation, from zero for the newest.
  • $\lambda$: decay.
  • $L$: hac_lags. Where hac_lags is a vector, $L$ is its length and $k_{i}$ its entries, such as the kernel of hac_row_kernel.

Algorithm

  1. Lay the grid of regime_bias_table.
  2. For each $K$, add one row of the banded LDLᵀ factorisation of $I + s B$ at every point of the grid with hac_ldl_row!, so $\ln G$ takes the log of the new pivot and the whole table costs one pass. A pivot that is not positive marks the point: past it, the determinant has crossed zero.
  3. Cut the transform at its minimum with hac_laplace!, and evaluate the factor of the method with regime_bias_factor.

Where the estimate is positive definite, the table agrees with the eigenvalues of $B$ on a grid of step $1/200$ on $[-75, 60]$ to $10^{-10}$, and with a Monte Carlo of 400 000 draws to its noise. Where it is not, the grid agrees with the finer one to $5 \times 10^{-7}$, and to $4 \times 10^{-4}$ at a half-life of 5 and five lags, where the estimate is negative in $6 \times 10^{-5}$ of draws.

Computes the exact bias factor of a regime statistic for every count from one to K, where each deviation is taken from an estimated location.

The location of each deviation holds the returns before it, so the terms of the estimate share their returns, and the estimate is a quadratic form $Q = c\, x^{\top} M x$ in the returns with a dense $M$. The law of $Q$ is then not the law of the pre-centred table. The next deviation $u$ reads the same location, so $u$ and $Q$ are not independent either. Each part moves the factor. Without HAC the two parts nearly cancel: at a half-life of 10 the pre-centred factors are within $4 \times 10^{-4}$ of this table. With HAC they add: there the pre-centred root-mean-squared factor is 0.26 %, 0.58 % and 1.39 % too large at one, two and four lags, as a Monte Carlo of the estimator confirms. This table is exact for both parts.

The estimate after $K$ terms is $R_{K} = \lambda R_{K-1} + q_{K}$ before its normalisation, so the Laplace transform at $s$ after $K$ terms reads the transform at $\lambda s$ after $K - 1$ terms. On a lattice of step $|\ln \lambda|$ in $\ln s$ that is the next point below, and a Gaussian state carries the location and the last deviations at each point, so each count costs one step at each point.

Algorithm

  1. Make the lattice with estimated_bias_lattice.
  2. For each count up to $K_{h} = \lceil \ln \sqrt{\epsilon} / \ln \lambda \rceil$, advance the lattice with estimated_bias_count!, take the integrals with estimated_bias_integrals!, and the factor with estimated_bias_factor.
  3. Past $K_{h}$ the factor changes as $\lambda^{K}$, and so does the pre-centred one. Each count takes the pre-centred factor times the ratio of the two at $K_{h}$.

Where the estimate is positive definite, the table agrees with the eigenvalues of a dense $M$ on a grid of step $1/200$ to $10^{-12}$. Where it is not, the cut moves with the lattice, as in regime_bias_table, and the table agrees with that grid to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ at the first indefinite counts of a half-life of 10 and two lags, and to $10^{-9}$ from 20 counts. The rule of step 3 is within $2 \times 10^{-11}$ of the recursion at a half-life of 40 and two lags, and within $4 \times 10^{-9}$ at a half-life of 5 and four lags. The table to the saturation count costs 0.1 s at a half-life of 40 and two lags.

Returns whether an estimate of K observations is old enough for its regime statistic to be scored.

The statistic of n directions reads the inverse of an estimate, and the variance of that inverse is finite from $K > n + 3$. Before that, one observation can hold the smoothed regime state for many half-lives. A HAC estimate has a weight matrix with negative eigenvalues, so it can fail to be positive definite at any count, and no moment of its inverse is finite. Its effective count of observations, $1 / \operatorname{tr}(A^{2})$ from exp_weight_cross_sum, must also pass $n + 1$, the count at which the mean of the inverse of a plain estimate is finite. At 12 assets, a half-life of 10 and two lags, K > n + 3 alone scores the estimate at $K = 16$, where 11 % of draws are not positive definite. With the effective count the first scored row is $K = 54$, and over half-lives of 5, 10 and 40, one to five lags and one to 12 assets, the first scored row is not positive definite in at most $2 \times 10^{-4}$ of draws. An estimate whose steady state has too few effective observations, such as 12 assets at a half-life of 5, is never scored. Without the correction every observation above min_obs is scored.

Read the regime-adjusted variance out of a cache, as it stands.

Applies the exponentially weighted bias correction, blanks every asset that is not ready, and scales by the square of the regime multiplier. A HAC state is a quadratic form whose weight matrix is indefinite, so it can fall below zero, and the variance is floored at zero here rather than in the recursion, which stays unbiased. The cache is read, never written, so the same cache answers this call after every observation of a forward pass.

Where ce.regime_method is nothing, process_observation! advances no regime state, so cache.n_regime_obs stays at zero, which is below every admissible regime_min_obs. The multiplier is then one and the variance is the plain recursion.

Where ce.regime_lohi_mult is not nothing, the multiplier is clamped to that (lo, hi) range before it is squared. Where it is nothing, no clamp runs. The clamp bounds an estimate, so the multiplier of one before regime_min_obs holds even where lo > 1 or hi < 1.

  • ce::RegimeAdjustedExpWeightedVariance: Variance estimator configuration.
