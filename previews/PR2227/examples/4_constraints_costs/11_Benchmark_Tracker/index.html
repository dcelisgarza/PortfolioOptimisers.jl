Benchmark tracker

A benchmark tracker is a portfolio whose returns follow the returns of an index as closely as possible. Its objective is the tracking error, the distance between the two series of returns. A fund that tracks an index often holds only a part of the index, because each position costs money to hold and to trade, so the tracker also has a cap on the number of assets.

The library states this problem with parts that already exist:

  • TrackingRiskMeasure with a ReturnsTracking benchmark measures the tracking error to a series of returns, such as an index;
  • MinimumRisk makes that measure the objective of MeanRisk;
  • the card keyword of JuMPOptimiser caps the number of assets that hold a weight.

The turnover and tracking example shows the tracking measures, and the cardinality and threshold example shows card. This page puts them together, and shows how the tracking error grows as the cap falls.

When to reach for this

Reach for a benchmark tracker when your mandate is to follow an index, and you cannot or do not want to hold every asset of the index. To beat the index within a limit instead, put the tracking error in a constraint, as section 5 of the turnover and tracking example does.

A mixed-integer solver is required

card adds binary variables, so the capped trackers need a mixed-integer solver.

2. The tracking error and the solver

TrackingRiskMeasure with a ReturnsTracking benchmark measures the distance between the returns of the portfolio and the returns of the index. Its alg field sets the norm of the daily differences. We use L1Norm, so the tracking error is the mean absolute difference between the daily return of the portfolio and the daily return of the index. It is in the units of a daily return.

With L1Norm, the tracker is a linear program, and the tracker with a cap is a mixed-integer linear program. HiGHS solves both.

The default norm is slower with a cap

The default norm, L2Norm, is the Euclidean norm of the daily differences divided by $\sqrt{T - 1}$, where $T$ is the number of days. It makes the capped tracker a mixed-integer conic problem, which needs a solver such as Pajarito, as in the cardinality and threshold example. On this data, one solve with card = 5 took 164 s with Pajarito. The same solve with L1Norm took 5 s with HiGHS, and the two portfolios had almost the same L2Norm tracking error.
