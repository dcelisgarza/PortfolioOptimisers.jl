OWA risk measures (a)

Several risk measures are ordered weighted averages. Such a measure sorts the portfolio returns, and weights each return by its rank [197, 205]. The higher L-moments are linear combinations of such averages [206].

The types and functions below build the weight vectors of these measures, and the formulations that optimise them.

abstract type AbstractOrderedWeightsArrayFunction <: AbstractEstimator

Abstract supertype for the callable builders of an OWA weight vector.

A builder holds the parameters of one weight vector, such as a CVaR level, and computes the vector for a sample size when it is called. OrderedWeightsArray and OrderedWeightsArrayRange call it at the number of observations of the sample, so one builder serves a sample of any length.

PortfolioOptimisers.MaximumEntropy — Type
struct MaximumEntropy{__T_alg} <: AbstractOrderedWeightsArrayAlgorithm

Selects the risk aversion coefficients of maximum entropy in the L-moment programme of OWAJuMP.

The objective is the Shannon entropy of the coefficients $\boldsymbol{\phi}$. The coefficients are a probability vector, so the objective spreads the weight over the L-moments as evenly as the rows let it. The objective is strictly concave, so the programme has one maximiser, and the OWA weights $\mathbf{W} \boldsymbol{\phi}$ are unique.

alg selects the conic encoding of the entropy, not the problem. RelativeEntropy writes one JuMP.MOI.RelativeEntropyCone of dimension 2K - 1, and ExponentialConeEntropy writes K - 1 JuMP.MOI.ExponentialCone rows.

Warning

Keep the objective scale so of OWAJuMP near 1e-3 for this objective. The default so = nothing gives 1e-3 through owa_objective_scale. A stated so = 1 makes the solver stop short of optimal, and owa_l_moment_crm then warns and returns the fallback weights. The steps of $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ are of order $1/T^{2}$, the entropy is of order one, and the maximiser touches the rows $\theta_{t+1} \geq \theta_{t}$ tangentially. With max_phi = 0.75, $T$ from 30 to 5000 and the highest order from 4 to 10, Clarabel solved 8 of 39 programmes at so = 1 and all 39 at so = 1e-3. On a grid of 20 of these programmes, so = 1e-2 solved 17 or 18, by encoding, and so = 1e-5 solved 19. The scale does not change the maximiser.

Equation 13 of [206] maximises $\sum_{t} \ln \psi_{t}$ over a vector $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ subject to $\psi_{t} \geq \lvert \theta_{t} \rvert$ alone, and that objective has no maximum. Its intent, the entropy of the absolute OWA weights, does not select one vector either, and it is not a convex programme in the normalised form. The L-moment weight vector of order $k$ has entries of absolute value at most $1/T$ once $T > k (k - 1) / 2$, measured for every order up to 10 and every $T$ up to 600. So every feasible $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ has $\lvert \theta_{t} \rvert \leq 1/T$, and the uniform $\psi_{t} = 1/T$ bounds it. A probability vector $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ that bounds $\lvert \boldsymbol{\theta} \rvert$ from above then has the largest entropy $\log T$ at every feasible point. The library puts the entropy on the coefficients for this reason.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \underset{\boldsymbol{\phi},\, \boldsymbol{\theta}}{\max} \quad & -\sum\limits_{k=2}^{K} \phi_{k} \log \phi_{k}\\ \text{s.t.} \quad & \boldsymbol{\theta} = \mathbf{W} \boldsymbol{\phi}\\ & \sum\limits_{k=2}^{K} \phi_{k} = 1\\ & 0 \leq \phi_{k} \leq \phi_{\max} \quad \forall k = 2,\, \ldots,\, K\\ & \phi_{k+1} \leq \phi_{k} \quad \forall k = 2,\, \ldots,\, K - 1\\ & \theta_{t+1} \geq \theta_{t} \quad \forall t = 1,\, \ldots,\, T - 1\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{\theta}$: Solved OWA weight vector $T \times 1$, the model variable theta.
  • $\mathbf{W}$: Signed L-moment weight matrix $T \times (K - 1)$. Its column $k - 1$ is $(-1)^{k} \boldsymbol{\omega}^{k}$ for $k = 2, \ldots, K$.
  • $\omega_{i}^{k}$: L-moment weight of the $i$-th smallest observation in the $k$-th sample L-moment, the $i$-th entry of owa_l_moment(T, k).
  • $\phi_{k}$: Risk aversion coefficient of the $k$-th L-moment, for $k = 2, \ldots, K$.
  • $\phi_{\max}$: Ceiling on each risk aversion coefficient, the max_phi field of OWAJuMP.
  • $K$: Highest L-moment order, the k of owa_l_moment_crm.
  • $T$: Number of observations.

struct MinimumSquaredDistance{__T_alg} <: SquaredOrderedWeightsArrayAlgorithm{__T_alg}

Selects the smoothest OWA weights in the L-moment programme of OWAJuMP.

The objective is the sum of the squared differences of adjacent OWA weights, so the weights change little from one order statistic to the next. Use MinimumSumSquares to keep the weights small instead.

alg selects the conic encoding of the objective, not the problem. PortfolioOptimisers.NormalisedConstantRelativeRiskAversion — Type
struct NormalisedConstantRelativeRiskAversion{__T_g} <: AbstractOrderedWeightsArrayEstimator

Sets the risk aversion coefficients of an L-moment OWA weight vector to the normalised constant relative risk aversion coefficients of g.

Equation 12 of [206] states the coefficients. The library divides them by their sum, which that equation suggests. It also replaces each OWA weight by the largest weight up to its position, so the vector is nondecreasing and the risk measure is convex. ncrra_weights computes the vector, and OWAJuMP falls back to it with g = 0.5 when its programme fails.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \tilde{\phi}_{k} &= \dfrac{1}{k!} \prod\limits_{j=0}^{k-2} \left(\gamma + j\right) \quad \forall k = 2,\, \ldots,\, K\\ \phi_{k} &= \dfrac{\tilde{\phi}_{k}}{\sum_{j=2}^{K} \tilde{\phi}_{j}}\\ \boldsymbol{\vartheta} &= \mathbf{W} \boldsymbol{\phi}\\ \eta_{t} &= \max\limits_{s \leq t} \vartheta_{s} \quad \forall t = 1,\, \ldots,\, T\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\gamma$: Relative risk aversion g of the normalised constant relative risk aversion coefficients, $\gamma \in (0, 1)$.
  • $\tilde{\phi}_{k}$: Coefficient of the $k$-th L-moment before the normalisation.
  • $\phi_{k}$: Risk aversion coefficient of the $k$-th L-moment, for $k = 2, \ldots, K$.
  • $\mathbf{W}$: Signed L-moment weight matrix $T \times (K - 1)$. Its column $k - 1$ is $(-1)^{k} \boldsymbol{\omega}^{k}$ for $k = 2, \ldots, K$.
  • $\omega_{i}^{k}$: L-moment weight of the $i$-th smallest observation in the $k$-th sample L-moment, the $i$-th entry of owa_l_moment(T, k).
  • $\boldsymbol{\vartheta}$: Combination of the signed L-moment weights, $T \times 1$. It need not be nondecreasing.
  • $\boldsymbol{\eta}$: OWA weight vector $T \times 1$. Its $t$-th entry $\eta_{t}$ multiplies the $t$-th smallest return.
  • $K$: Highest L-moment order, the k of owa_l_moment_crm.
  • $T$: Number of observations.

The coefficients sum to one, and the OWA weights do not. Each L-moment weight vector of order two or more sums to zero, so $\boldsymbol{\vartheta}$ sums to zero, and the running maximum can only raise that sum.

PortfolioOptimisers.ExactOrderedWeightsArray — Type
struct ExactOrderedWeightsArray <: OrderedWeightsArrayFormulation

Encodes the exact OWA risk with the linear programme that sorts the sample.

The programme is the dual of the assignment problem that pairs each weight with an order statistic. So it sorts the sample inside the model, and the sort costs T^2 rows. It adds two vector variables a and b of length T and the T × T block of rows $y_{i} w_{j} \leq a_{j} + b_{i}$, and the risk is $\sum_{t} (a_{t} + b_{t})$. The method of set_risk_constraints! for this formulation states the model. For a long sample, SortingNetworkOrderedWeightsArray gives the same exact risk at $O(T \log^{2} T)$ rows, and ApproxOrderedWeightsArray gives an upper bound at a lower cost.

The programme pairs the largest weight with the largest sorted return, so it attains $\mathrm{sort}(\boldsymbol{\omega})^{\intercal} \mathrm{sort}(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}})$. This is the OWA risk when, and only when, the weight vector is nondecreasing, which is also the condition for the risk measure to be convex. Every weight builder in this package returns such a vector. The programme sorts a weight vector that is out of order, so the model and the functor then disagree.

struct ApproxOrderedWeightsArray{__T_p} <: OrderedWeightsArrayFormulation

Encodes an upper bound on the OWA risk with one power cone block for each p-norm order in p.

It relaxes the dual representation of the OWA risk, and does not sort. It replaces the T × T block of ExactOrderedWeightsArray by one p-norm row for each entry of p. It keeps only the properties of the weight vector that a reordering leaves unchanged: the minimum, the maximum, the sum, and one p-norm for each entry of p. Every reordering of the weight vector meets those properties, so the model risk lies at or above the exact OWA risk of the same portfolio. The method of set_risk_constraints! for this formulation states the model and its ## Relaxation.

The bound is exact when at most one entry of the weight vector lies strictly between its minimum and its maximum, as the vectors of owa_wr and of owa_cvar at every $\alpha$ do. The box and the sum then describe the convex hull of the reorderings, so the model omits the p-norm cones and is a linear programme. There the cones are redundant, and they put the optimum at the apex of every cone, where an interior point solver can stall. For other vectors the gap closes as the weight vector approaches a line. Cajas [205] studies the linear case. For the Gini mean difference and the tail Gini over samples of 500 to 10,000 observations, [205] reports the same objective value as the exact formulation, to the printed precision. Measured against the functor at T = 100, N = 8 with the default p, the Gini mean difference is 0.06 % high, the tail Gini is 1.7e-5 % high, and the tail Gini range is 0.37 % high. A fourth-order L-moment weight vector, which is not linear, is 4.5 % high. This formulation is the fastest of the three. For a weight vector that is far from a line, prefer an exact formulation: SortingNetworkOrderedWeightsArray for a long sample, and ExactOrderedWeightsArray for a short one.

struct OWAJuMP{__T_slv, __T_max_phi, __T_sc, __T_so, __T_alg} <: AbstractOrderedWeightsArrayEstimator

Solves a programme for the risk aversion coefficients of an L-moment OWA weight vector.

alg selects the objective, and every objective solves over the same feasible set. owa_l_moment_crm states the programme. sc scales the rows and so scales the objective, and neither changes the solution. A stated scale wins. When a scale is nothing, the objective gives it through owa_constraint_scale and owa_objective_scale. So the default so is 1e-3 under MaximumEntropy, which needs a small so to solve, as its docstring states, and 1.0 under the other objectives.

When the solver fails, owa_model_solve warns and returns the weights of NormalisedConstantRelativeRiskAversion(; g = 0.5). The programme is infeasible for some max_phi and some orders: the coefficients sum to one and each is at most max_phi, and the OWA weights must be nondecreasing. At k = 2 it is always infeasible, because its one coefficient must equal one. The fallback then returns the weights of the second L-moment, which is the only answer whose coefficients sum to one. At k = 3 with the default max_phi = 0.5, the rows leave the one choice $\boldsymbol{\phi} = (0.5, 0.5)$, and its OWA weights decrease near the largest return, so the programme is infeasible too.

owa_l_moment_crm(method::AbstractOrderedWeightsArrayEstimator, weights::MatNum)

Combine the columns of a signed L-moment weight matrix into one OWA weight vector, with the risk aversion coefficients that method finds.

NormalisedConstantRelativeRiskAversion states the coefficients in closed form, through ncrra_weights. OWAJuMP builds the feasible set with owa_model_setup, adds the rows and the objective of its alg, and solves the model with owa_model_solve. The docstrings of MaximumEntropy, MinimumSquaredDistance and MinimumSumSquares state the three programmes.

Algorithm

For an OWAJuMP method:

  1. Build model, the feasible set, with owa_model_setup.
  2. Add the variables and the rows of the objective of method.alg, as # JuMP formulation states. Under MaximumEntropy, owa_l_moment_crm_entropy adds them.
  3. Set the objective, with owa_l_moment_crm_entropy for MaximumEntropy and with owa_l_moment_crm_sumsq_obj otherwise.
  4. Solve model and return its OWA weights, with owa_model_solve.

JuMP formulation

Variables

  • theta: Read, the OWA weights that owa_model_setup creates.
  • t: Created under MinimumSquaredDistance and MinimumSumSquares, the scalar that the cone bounds. Under MaximumEntropy, owa_l_moment_crm_entropy creates it.

Constraints

Every row is registered under no name. Under MaximumEntropy the method adds no row, and owa_l_moment_crm_entropy adds the cone rows of the entropy.

  • Under MinimumSquaredDistance with SOCRiskExpr or SquaredSOCRiskExpr: $(s_c t,\, s_c \boldsymbol{d}) \in \mathcal{K}_{2}$, which states $t \geq \lVert \boldsymbol{d} \rVert_{2}$.
  • Under MinimumSquaredDistance with RSOCRiskExpr: $(s_c t,\, s_c / 2,\, s_c \boldsymbol{d}) \in \mathcal{Q}_{r}$, which states $t \geq \lVert \boldsymbol{d} \rVert_{2}^{2}$.
  • Under MinimumSumSquares with SOCRiskExpr or SquaredSOCRiskExpr: $(s_c t,\, s_c \boldsymbol{\theta}) \in \mathcal{K}_{2}$, which states $t \geq \lVert \boldsymbol{\theta} \rVert_{2}$.
  • Under MinimumSumSquares with RSOCRiskExpr: $(s_c t,\, s_c / 2,\, s_c \boldsymbol{\theta}) \in \mathcal{Q}_{r}$, which states $t \geq \lVert \boldsymbol{\theta} \rVert_{2}^{2}$.

                 method::AbstractOrderedWeightsArrayEstimator = NormalisedConstantRelativeRiskAversion();

Compute the OWA weight vector of the L-moment convex risk measure of the orders 2 to k.

The risk measure is a signed combination of the L-moments of the portfolio returns, with the risk aversion coefficients that method finds. The coefficients sum to one, and the OWA weights do not, because each L-moment weight vector of order two or more sums to zero.

Each signed L-moment weight vector starts at $-1/T$, so the first OWA weight is $-1/T$ at every order and for every method. A higher k moves the other entries. Under the default method on 60 observations, the largest move is $0.61/T$ from k = 2 to k = 3, and $0.053/T$ from k = 7 to k = 8.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \rho(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \sum\limits_{k=2}^{K} \left(-1\right)^{k} \phi_{k} \lambda_{k}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}\right)\\ &= \sum\limits_{i=1}^{T} \eta_{i} \hat{r}_{[i]}\\ \eta_{i} &= \sum\limits_{k=2}^{K} \left(-1\right)^{k} \phi_{k} \omega_{i}^{k}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\rho(\boldsymbol{w})$: L-moment convex risk measure of the portfolio.
  • $\lambda_{k}(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}})$: The $k$-th sample L-moment of the net portfolio returns.
  • $\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}$: The net portfolio returns, $T \times 1$, with entries $\hat{r}_t$. $\hat{r}_{[i]}$ is its $i$-th smallest entry.
  • $\phi_{k}$: Risk aversion coefficient of the $k$-th L-moment, for $k = 2, \ldots, K$.
  • $\omega_{i}^{k}$: L-moment weight of the $i$-th smallest observation in the $k$-th sample L-moment, the $i$-th entry of owa_l_moment(T, k).
  • $\boldsymbol{\eta}$: OWA weight vector $T \times 1$. Its $t$-th entry $\eta_{t}$ multiplies the $t$-th smallest return.
  • $K$: Highest L-moment order, the k of owa_l_moment_crm.
  • $T$: Number of observations.

Algorithm

  1. Check that k >= 2.
  2. Build weights, the signed L-moment weight matrix $T \times (k - 1)$. Its column i - 1 is $(-1)^{i}$ times owa_l_moment(T, i), for i from 2 to k.
  3. Return owa_l_moment_crm(method, weights), which finds the coefficients and combines the columns.

Arguments

  • T: Number of observations.
  • method: Estimator of the risk aversion coefficients.
  • k: Highest L-moment order.

Validation

  • k >= 2. Raises a DomainError.
  • k <= T, through owa_l_moment. Raises a DomainError.

Returns

  • w::VecNum: Vector of OWA weights of length T.

Compute the OWA weight vector of the sample L-moment of order k.

The k-th sample L-moment is a weighted sum of the order statistics of the sample, so it is an OWA operator, and this function returns its weights. For k >= 2 the weights sum to zero, and for k = 1 they are $1/T$, the weights of the mean.

The binomial coefficients that hold $T$ are computed in the element type of the weights, because $\binom{T}{k}$ exceeds the largest Int at T = 1000 and k = 8. On a Float64 vector the weights agree with exact rational arithmetic to a relative error below 1e-14 up to k = 10.

The vector is the loss-tail vector at level alpha less the reversed loss-tail vector at level beta. The reversal moves the second tail to the largest returns, and the subtraction makes its weights positive. So the risk is the conditional value at risk of the losses plus the mean of the beta share of the largest returns, a sum of the two tails. The vector sums to zero.

Note

Under ApproxOrderedWeightsArray, an OrderedWeightsArray over this fused vector is a looser relaxation than the two tails of an OrderedWeightsArrayRange, which OWA_CVaR_RG builds. In a minimum-risk MeanRisk on seeded returns with $T = 100$, $N = 8$ and $\alpha = \beta = 0.05$, the fused optimum has an exact risk 4.6 % above the exact optimum, and the two tails reach the exact optimum. Under ExactOrderedWeightsArray and SortingNetworkOrderedWeightsArray, the two spellings give the same model.
