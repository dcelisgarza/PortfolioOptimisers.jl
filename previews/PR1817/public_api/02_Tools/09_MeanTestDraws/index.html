Mean test draws

Draw algorithms

PortfolioOptimisers.ObservationDraws — Type
struct ObservationDraws <: AbstractBootstrapDraws

Draws one multiplier for each finite entry of the series, and computes the statistic of each draw from the product.

It reads every kernel at its exact weights. Every kernel reads the multipliers in the same order, one per finite entry and draw by draw, so two kernels answer their p-values from the same draws of one generator. Under BartlettKernel a draw reads sums of windows, so its cost is linear in the length of the series. Under any other kernel a block of draws is one product with the matrix of the weights, so the cost of a draw is quadratic in the length. It is the default of every kernel except QuadraticSpectralKernel.

PortfolioOptimisers.QuadratureSumDraws — Type
struct QuadratureSumDraws{__T_tol, __T_nodes, __T_form} <: AbstractBootstrapDraws

Draws the statistic of each draw from a few Gaussian sums, through quadrature features of the QuadraticSpectralKernel.

The quadratic spectral kernel is the cosine transform of a weight on a bounded band, so a Gauss-Legendre rule of $2N$ nodes on the band writes the matrix of its weights as $\mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{\Phi}^{\intercal}$, with $2N$ features per entry. The statistic of a draw reads the multipliers only through the sum of the product and its sums against the centred features. These $2N + 1$ sums are jointly Gaussian, with the covariance $\mathbf{H}^{\intercal} \mathbf{H}$, so the test draws them directly. A draw then costs $O(N^{2})$, whatever the length of the series.

The observed statistic reads the weighted sum of the observed series by the rule form. The default FeatureLongRunForm reads it through the same features, so the whole test costs $O(P K)$ once and $O(K^{2})$ per draw, with $K = 2N$. ExactLongRunForm reads the exact sum over every lag, at $O(P^{2})$.

The largest angle that the test reads, $c$, sets the number of nodes. Under FiniteEntrySpan, $c < 6 \pi / (5 b)$, so the count depends on the bandwidth fraction b and not on the length of the series. The count is the smallest $N$ whose bound on the error of the quadrature, at every lag, is at most tol. The bound is that of Gauss quadrature for a function analytic in the Bernstein ellipse $E_{\rho}$, minimised over $\rho$. Measured at a tolerance of 1e-14, it agrees with the smallest sufficient count to one node: 9 nodes at b = 0.4, 19 at b = 0.1 and 114 at b = 0.01. A fixed count is wrong at a small b: 32 nodes give an error of 0.57 at b = 0.02.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} k(x) &\approx \sum_{l = 1}^{N} a_{l}^{2} \cos\left( \frac{6 \pi}{5} x u_{l} \right)\,, \quad a_{l} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2} w_{l} \left( 1 - u_{l}^{2} \right)}\,, \\ \boldsymbol{\phi}_{i} &= \left( a_{l} \cos(\theta_{i} u_{l}),\, a_{l} \sin(\theta_{i} u_{l}) \right)_{l = 1}^{N}\,, \quad \theta_{i} = \frac{6 \pi (i - 1)}{5 B}\,, \\ \boldsymbol{h}_{i} &= d_{i} \left( 1,\, \boldsymbol{\phi}_{i} - \bar{\boldsymbol{\phi}} \right)\,, \quad \mathbf{H}^{\intercal} \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{R}^{\intercal} \mathbf{R}\,, \\ \boldsymbol{v} &= \mathbf{R}^{\intercal} \boldsymbol{z}\,, \quad \boldsymbol{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0}, \mathbf{I})\,, \quad \bar{u}^{*} = \frac{v_{1}}{n}\,, \quad Q^{*} = \sum_{k \geq 2} v_{k}^{2}\,, \\ \epsilon_{N} &\leq \min_{\rho > 1} \frac{16}{5} \left( 1 + \alpha_{\rho}^{2} \right) \cosh\left( c \beta_{\rho} \right) \frac{\rho^{-4N}}{\rho^{2} - 1}\,, \quad c = \frac{6 \pi (P - 1)}{5 B}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $u_{l}$, $w_{l}$: The positive nodes and their weights of the Gauss-Legendre rule of $2N$ nodes on $[-1, 1]$, from the eigenvalues of its Jacobi matrix.
  • $a_{l}$: The amplitude of node $l$.
  • $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{i}$: The features of entry $i$ of the span, and $\bar{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ their mean over the finite entries.
  • $B$: The bandwidth, in entries.
  • $d_{i}$: Entry $i$ of the centred series. A missing entry has no row.
  • $\mathbf{R}$: A square-root factor of $\mathbf{H}^{\intercal} \mathbf{H}$, from its eigenvalues above round-off and their eigenvectors.
  • $\bar{u}^{*}$, $Q^{*}$: The mean and the weighted sum of the centred product of a draw, which studentised_mean reads.
  • $\epsilon_{N}$: The largest error of the quadrature over the lags of the span.
  • $\alpha_{\rho} = (\rho + \rho^{-1}) / 2$, $\beta_{\rho} = (\rho - \rho^{-1}) / 2$: The semi-axes of $E_{\rho}$.
  • $P$: The length of the span, from the first finite entry to the last.

Fields

  • tol: Bound on the error of the quadrature at every lag that the test reads, in (0, 1), or nothing for the machine epsilon of the kernel weights. The weight at lag zero is one, so the bound is relative to the largest weight.
  • nodes: Number of positive nodes of the rule, or nothing to derive it from tol. A count overrides the derivation, and a count below the derived one gives a kernel with an error above tol.
  • form: Rule that computes the weighted sum of the observed series, an AbstractLongRunForm.

struct EigenSumDraws <: AbstractBootstrapDraws

Draws the statistic of each draw from a few Gaussian sums, through an eigen factor of the matrix of the kernel weights.

The algorithm factors the symmetric matrix $\mathbf{W}$ of the weights of every pair once, as $\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{\Phi}_{+} \mathbf{\Phi}_{+}^{\intercal} - \mathbf{\Phi}_{-} \mathbf{\Phi}_{-}^{\intercal}$, from its eigenvalues above round-off in absolute value. It then draws the Gaussian sums of QuadratureSumDraws with these columns as the features, and subtracts the squares of the sums that read $\mathbf{\Phi}_{-}$. The factor is exact, so it reads every kernel, one that a user writes included, and a kernel whose weights are not positive semidefinite. The factorisation costs $O(P^{3})$ once, and a draw costs $O(r^{2})$, with $r$ the rank of the factor. An eigenvalue at most $P \epsilon \max_{k} |\lambda_{k}|$ in absolute value is round-off of zero, and the factor drops it.

Observed long-run forms

PortfolioOptimisers.AbstractLongRunForm — Type
abstract type AbstractLongRunForm <: AbstractAlgorithm

Abstract supertype for the rules that compute the weighted sum of the observed series under QuadratureSumDraws.

The observed t-statistic reads $Q = \boldsymbol{d}^{\intercal} \mathbf{W} \boldsymbol{d}$, the sum of the products of every pair of entries of the centred series weighted by the kernel. The quadratic spectral kernel weights every lag, so the exact sum costs $O(P^{2})$: 1.2 s at 50,000 entries. Through the features of the draws, $\mathbf{W} \approx \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{\Phi}^{\intercal}$, the sum is $\lVert \mathbf{\Phi}^{\intercal} \boldsymbol{d} \rVert^{2}$ and costs $O(P K)$: 0.8 ms at 50,000 entries.

Interfaces

struct FeatureLongRunForm <: AbstractLongRunForm

Computes the weighted sum of the observed series through the quadrature features of the draws, as $\lVert \mathbf{\Phi}^{\intercal} \boldsymbol{d} \rVert^{2}$.

Its cost is linear in the length of the series, and the observed statistic reads the same kernel as the draws. It differs from the exact sum by the error of the quadrature: 1.3e-14 relative at 50,000 entries at the default tolerance. It is the default of QuadratureSumDraws.
