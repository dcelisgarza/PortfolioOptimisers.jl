PortfolioOptimisers.is_basis_invariant — Function
is_basis_invariant(tgt::Union{LinearModel, GeneralisedLinearModel}) -> Bool
is_basis_invariant(tgt::AbstractRegressionTarget) -> Bool

Return whether the fit of a regression target gives the same coefficients under an invertible change of the columns of the design, mapped back to the original columns.

Take the columns of the design times an invertible matrix as a new design. A target is invariant to the basis when its coefficients on the new design, times that matrix, are its coefficients on the old design. Then the fitted values do not change either.

A weighted least-squares fit projects the response onto the span of the design, and the change keeps the span. So LinearModel returns true, on each design of full rank. A maximum-likelihood fit reads the design through the linear predictor alone, and the change keeps the set of linear predictors, so GeneralisedLinearModel returns true too.

The fit of GeneralisedLinearModel is iterative, but each iteration is a weighted least-squares fit, and the start reads the response alone. The two bases then take the same iterates and stop at the same one, and they agree to rounding. GLM stops when the change in the deviance falls below max(rtol * deviance, atol), with rtol = 1e-6, atol = 1e-6 and maxiter = 30 by default. The kwargs of the target set the three values. A tolerance near the rounding of the deviance lets rounding stop the two bases one iteration apart, and the two answers then differ by that iteration. A tighter tolerance brings each fit nearer to the maximum-likelihood answer, and it does not bring the two bases nearer to each other.

On a rank-deficient design, GLM drops a collinear column by a pivot, so the coefficients depend on the basis. A caller that reads this method, such as cross_sectional_target_coef, fits such a design in a pivoted basis of its own. Every other target returns false, because this method cannot know whether such a target penalises its coefficients.

The carry fold of CrossSectionalFactorPrior reads the method on the target of a CrossSectionalTargetRegression, when a BatchChoice moves the dropped member of a Factor Family. A target of the caller states its answer with its own method, as the # Interfaces section of AbstractRegressionTarget states.

Arguments

  • tgt: The regression target.

Returns

  • flag::Bool: true when the coefficients of the fit do not depend on the basis of a design of full rank.

struct GeneralisedLinearModel{__T_args, __T_kwargs, __T_variant} <: AbstractUnpenalisedRegressionTarget

Fits each response by a generalised linear model through GLM.GeneralizedLinearModel.

The args field carries the response distribution and, as an option, the link function. The kwargs field carries the other GLM options. The default args = (Normal(),) with the canonical identity link gives ordinary least squares.

GLM defines $R^2$ for a fitted LinearModel only, and variant names the pseudo-$R^2$ that the :r2 and :adjr2 members of STEPWISE_REGRESSION_CRITERIA read in its place. A nothing variant takes the default of the criterion, which default_regression_criterion_variant states. A minimisation criterion reads no variant, so it ignores the field.

The fit is iterative, and kwargs sets when it stops. GLM stops when the change in the deviance falls below max(rtol * deviance, atol), with rtol = 1e-6, atol = 1e-6 and maxiter = 30 by default. A tighter tolerance, such as kwargs = (; rtol = 1e-12, atol = 1e-12), brings the fit nearer to the maximum-likelihood answer, and needs more iterations. A tolerance below the rounding of the deviance makes GLM throw.

is_basis_invariant returns true, so the carry fold of CrossSectionalFactorPrior folds a move of the dropped member of a Factor Family. The fold keeps the coefficients of the old basis, and the batch fit iterates in the new basis. The two take the same iterates and agree to rounding. When the tolerance is near the rounding of the deviance, rounding can stop the two one iteration apart at an observation. The two then differ by that iteration.

struct Regression{__T_M, __T_L, __T_b, __T_esigma, __T_edof, __T_ediv, __T_idx, __T_ts} <: AbstractLoadingsRegressionResult

Holds the loadings matrix, the intercept vector, the reduced-basis loadings and the idiosyncratic covariance of a fitted factor model.

M and b are the loadings matrix and the intercept vector of the factor model, with one row per asset. L holds the loadings in the reduced basis that a dimension reduction made. It is unset when the estimator regresses on the original factors.

An unset L reads back as M. A @forward_properties swap(L, M) rule makes re.L return re.M when the caller gave no L. So a consumer that decomposes risk in the factor basis needs no Nothing branch, and isnothing(re.L) is never true. Read getfield(re, :L) to tell the unset case apart, as port_opt_view does. StepwiseRegression leaves L unset and DimensionReductionRegression sets it. size(L, 2) is then the width of the basis of the risk decomposition, which is the number of original factors under the first, and the number of retained principal components under the second.

esigma holds the idiosyncratic covariance that the fit leaves. It has the same name and the same shapes as the field of CrossSectionalFactorModel, so one reader reads either block. A regression estimator fits loadings alone and writes nothing here. The prior that lifts the factor moments fills the field, and only when that prior adds a residual block. FactorPrior and FactorBlackLittermanPrior write the residual variances of factor_lift under rsd = true, and leave the field unset under rsd = false.

edof and ediv record the count behind each idiosyncratic variance. So a consumer that corrects for the sampling error of esigma, such as ResidualInflation, reads both fields from the block, and does not guess the count. StepwiseRegression and DimensionReductionRegression write edof as the number of observations less the parameters that the fit of each asset spent, which include the intercept. The prior that writes esigma restates edof in the effective count of its variance estimator. It also writes ediv, the divisor of each variance in that count, with variance_count. A block that the caller builds by hand, or that a variance estimator with no count measures, leaves both unset.

idx and ts are the row key of the block. The loadings are one fit over the sample, so the block has no rows of its own. The key names the observations of the factor returns, and of the reconstruction of the prior result that carries the block. FactorPrior writes the key. A block that the caller builds by hand, or that another prior builds, leaves it unset. A realised factor_attribution of a cross-validation reads the key to find the observations of each fold.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \boldsymbol{x}_{t} &= \boldsymbol{b} + \mathbf{M} \boldsymbol{f}_{t} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{x}_t$: Asset returns for observation $t$, the $t$-th row of the returns matrix.
  • $\boldsymbol{b}$: Intercept vector $N \times 1$, b. The term is absent when b is unset.
  • $\mathbf{M}$: Loadings matrix $N \times K$ of the factor model, M.
  • $\boldsymbol{f}_{t}$: Factor returns for observation $t$, the $t$-th row of the factor matrix.
  • $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t}$: Residual returns for observation $t$, the part of $\boldsymbol{x}_{t}$ that the factors do not explain.
  • $N$: Number of assets.
  • $K$: Number of factors.

Fields

  • M: M: Loadings matrix assets × factors.
  • L: L: Loadings matrix assets × reduced_dimensions in the reduced basis of a dimension reduction.
  • b: b: Regression intercept vector.
  • esigma: esigma: Idiosyncratic covariance. A vector holds the variances alone, and a matrix holds the full covariance.
  • edof: edof: Degrees of freedom of each residual series, one entry per asset. It is the effective count of the observations that measure the variance of the asset, less the parameters that the fit spent on the asset. A regression estimator writes it over equally weighted observations, and a prior that writes esigma restates it in the count of its variance estimator.
  • ediv: ediv: Divisor of each idiosyncratic variance, one entry per asset, in the effective count of edof. The variance is the weighted residual sum of squares in that count, divided by this entry. A prior that writes esigma writes it.
  • idx: Position of each observation that the prior result describes, in the returns data that the prior read, or nothing. A Scenario Cap keeps the last observations alone, so the positions are then the last positions of the data.
  • ts: Timestamp of each observation that idx names, or nothing when the returns data that the prior read carries no timestamps. When the folds of a cross-validation carry timestamps too, a factor_attribution matches the two by timestamp, so a prior fitted on other returns data still finds its observations.

Fit a time-series regression estimator to the asset returns and the factor returns of a ReturnsResult.

The method calls regression(re, X, rd.F) on the observations that it keeps. A Regression result is not an estimator, so it does not reach this method. The regression method of a result returns the result unchanged.

Algorithm

  1. Check that rd carries both matrices, as # Validation states.
  2. Read the source of re with returns_source, and call it x_src. A regression that reads the returns data itself states its source in its x_src field.
  3. With shared_series_view, drop the observations at which a factor return is not finite. Under x_src = ExcessSource(), also drop the observations of a gap of a vector benchmark. A regression on such an observation has no regressor for any asset.

PortfolioOptimisers.port_opt_view — Method
port_opt_view(re::Regression, i)

Return a view of a Regression result that keeps the assets that i selects.

The new result restricts the loadings matrix M, the optional reduced-basis loadings L, the intercept vector b, the idiosyncratic covariance esigma and the counts edof and ediv to the selected assets.

Algorithm

  1. Read L and b with getfield, never through property access. The swap(L, M) rule of Regression makes re.L return re.M when L is unset. So a property read puts a view of M in the L field of the new result, and the new result no longer shows that L was unset.
  2. Take a row view of M over i. These are the loadings of the selected assets.
  3. Take a row view of L over i when step 1 found a matrix, and nothing otherwise.
  4. Take an element view of b over i when step 1 found a vector, and nothing otherwise.
  5. View esigma with idiosyncratic_covariance_view, which reads its shape: a vector of variances is indexed once, and a full covariance is indexed on both axes.
  6. View edof and ediv with nothing_scalar_array_view, which indexes a vector once and passes nothing through.
  7. Build a new Regression from the six views and the unchanged row key idx and ts. The constructor runs all its guards again. The key names observations, so an asset selection does not change it.

Arguments

  • re: The regression result.
  • i: The indices of the assets to keep.

Returns

  • reg::Regression: A new regression result. Each field with one entry per asset holds the selected assets alone.
