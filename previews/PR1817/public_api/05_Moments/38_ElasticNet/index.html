Elastic net

ElasticNet is a penalised regression target. It fits a least-squares regression with a penalty on the $L_1$ norm and on the squared $L_2$ norm of the coefficients, and every estimator that takes a regression target can fit it. The strength of the penalty comes from a rule: MiddleOfPath reads it off the data at the middle of the regularisation path, and FixedStrength takes it from you. A new rule subtypes AbstractPenaltyStrength and adds a method of penalty_strength.

struct MiddleOfPath{__T_ratio} <: AbstractPenaltyStrength

Sets the regularisation strength of an ElasticNet at the geometric middle of its regularisation path.

Lai et al. [97] take "the middle one in the regularization path" and state no grid. Section 2.5 of [104] runs the path over 100 strengths on a log scale, from the smallest strength at which every penalised coefficient is zero down to ratio times that strength. Such a grid has no single middle point. The rule takes the geometric middle of the path, which lies between the 50th and the 51st point of that grid at the default ratio = 1e-3.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \gamma_{\max} &= \max_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \frac{\lvert \mathbf{P}_k^\intercal \mathbf{V} \boldsymbol{r}_0 \rvert}{\vartheta}\,,\\ \gamma &= \gamma_{\max} \sqrt{\rho}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\gamma_{\max}$: Smallest strength at which every penalised coefficient is zero.
  • $\mathcal{K}$: Penalised columns of the elastic net, every column of $\mathbf{P}$ but the intercept column that an estimator names.
  • $\mathbf{P}$: Regressors of the elastic net, one row per observation and one column per regressor. Column $k$ is $\mathbf{P}_k$.
  • $\mathbf{V}$: Diagonal matrix of the weights of the observations of the elastic net, the identity when the fit has no observation weights.
  • $\boldsymbol{r}_0$: Residual of the start point, the response less the fit of the intercept column alone, or the response when the design has no intercept column.
  • $\vartheta$: Share of the $L_1$ penalty in the elastic net, the theta field of ElasticNet.
  • $\gamma$: Regularisation strength of the elastic net.
  • $\rho$: Floor of the path as a fraction of $\gamma_{\max}$, the ratio field.

Every penalised coefficient is zero at and above $\gamma_{\max}$. The path runs from $\gamma_{\max}$ down to $\rho\, \gamma_{\max}$ on a log scale, so its geometric middle is $\gamma_{\max} \sqrt{\rho}$. A ridge alone, $\vartheta = 0$, has no $\gamma_{\max}$, so the constructor of ElasticNet refuses theta = 0 with this rule.

Fields

  • ratio: The floor of the path as a fraction of the strength above which every penalised coefficient is zero.

struct FixedStrength{__T_gamma} <: AbstractPenaltyStrength

Sets the regularisation strength of an ElasticNet to a value that the caller states.

gamma is the $\gamma$ of the objective of ElasticNet. It weighs the penalty against the sum of the weighted squared residuals, and it does not divide that sum by the number of observations. So the same gamma penalises a fit over more observations less, in relation to its residuals. Friedman et al. [104] divide the objective by twice the sum of the weights, so their strength $\lambda$ of Equation (1) is $\gamma / T_{e}$, with $T_{e}$ the sum of the scaled weights, which is the number of observations of an unweighted fit. With a variance estimator in the ve field of the target, gamma acts on the standardised columns. theta = 0 with this rule is a ridge regression.

PortfolioOptimisers.ElasticNet — Type
struct ElasticNet{__T_theta, __T_strength, __T_ve, __T_iters, __T_tol, __T_w, __T_ic} <: AbstractPenalisedRegressionTarget

Fits each response by the elastic net, a least-squares fit with a penalty on the $L_1$ norm and on the squared $L_2$ norm of the coefficients.

The objective is Equation (15) of [97], the elastic net of Zou and Hastie [105], under observation weights. It is Equation (3) of [105] with $\lambda_1 = 2 \vartheta \gamma$ and $\lambda_2 = (1 - \vartheta) \gamma$, so $\vartheta$ is the mixing weight of Equation (2) of [104], not the ratio $\lambda_2 / (\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)$ of [105]. Equation (10) of [104] is the coordinate update. strength sets $\gamma$: MiddleOfPath, the default, reads it off the data as Lai et al. [97] do, and FixedStrength takes it from the caller. theta = 0.99 is the value of [97]. It is almost the lasso, and it keeps the objective strictly convex.

The target adds no intercept and does not standardise the columns by default, as in the model of [97]. The glmnet package does both by default. An estimator that adds a column of ones names it with InterceptColumn through factory, and the fit then leaves that coefficient out of the penalty. A variance estimator in ve divides each penalised column by its standard deviation before the fit, and the fit scales the coefficients back. A column whose standard deviation is zero has no scale, so it keeps its units. ve reads the observation weights of the target, so the scale and the fit weigh the rows alike.

The observation weights enter as the frequency weights of glm_weights, the weights that LinearModel passes to GLM. Weights that are not StatsBase.FrequencyWeights then sum to their Kish effective sample size. Uniform weights give the unweighted fit at the same strength. The scale of the weights moves the fit under FixedStrength alone, because MiddleOfPath scales with them.

When the columns are almost collinear, for example the levels of consecutive periods in KernelTrendPattern, the coordinate sweeps converge slowly. Each sweep therefore ends with an exact solve on the sign pattern that it leaves, see elastic_net_polish. The sweeps must still find the sign pattern of the optimum. On the 4 × 5 window of the test fixture of KernelTrendPattern they find it after between 2000 and 5000 sweeps, and a cap of 1000 stops more than 0.5 from the optimum in one coefficient. When the sweeps stop at tol or at iters before that, the result is the last sweep, which is not the optimum.

The penalty depends on the units of each column, so the fit is not invariant to the basis of the design, and is_basis_invariant returns false. StepwiseRegression refuses the target, because its criteria count one degree of freedom for each coefficient.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \mathbf{P} &= \mathbf{X} \mathbf{S}^{-1}\,,\\ \hat{\boldsymbol{z}} &= \underset{\boldsymbol{z}}{\arg\min} \left(\boldsymbol{y} - \mathbf{P} \boldsymbol{z}\right)^\intercal \mathbf{V} \left(\boldsymbol{y} - \mathbf{P} \boldsymbol{z}\right) + \gamma \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \left( 2 \vartheta \lvert z_k \rvert + (1 - \vartheta) z_k^2 \right)\,,\\ \hat{\boldsymbol{b}} &= \mathbf{S}^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\mathbf{X}$: Design matrix of the caller, one row per observation and one column per regressor.
  • $\mathbf{S}$: Diagonal matrix of the standard deviations of the columns of $\mathbf{X}$ under ve, with one in place of the intercept column and of a column of zero standard deviation. It is the identity when ve is nothing.
  • $\mathbf{P}$: Regressors of the elastic net, one row per observation and one column per regressor. Column $k$ is $\mathbf{P}_k$.
  • $\boldsymbol{y}$: Response of the elastic net, one entry per observation.
  • $\boldsymbol{z}$: Coefficients of the elastic net, one per column of $\mathbf{P}$. Coefficient $k$ is $z_k$.
  • $\mathbf{V}$: Diagonal matrix of the weights of the observations of the elastic net, the identity when the fit has no observation weights. Its diagonal holds the frequency weights of glm_weights.
  • $\mathcal{K}$: Penalised columns of the elastic net, every column of $\mathbf{P}$ but the intercept column that an estimator names.
  • $\gamma$: Regularisation strength of the elastic net.
  • $\vartheta$: Share of the $L_1$ penalty in the elastic net, the theta field of ElasticNet.
  • $\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}$: Coefficients of the fit on the columns of $\mathbf{X}$, which StatsAPI.coef returns.

Fields

  • theta: The share of the $L_1$ penalty, 1 for the lasso and 0 for the ridge.
  • strength: The rule that sets the regularisation strength.
  • ve: Variance estimator. It standardises each penalised column, or nothing to fit the columns as they are.
  • iters: Maximum number of coordinate-descent sweeps. Each sweep ends with the exact solve on its sign pattern.
  • tol: Tolerance on the largest change of a coefficient over one sweep. The sweeps stop below it when no exact solve has given the optimum.
  • w: Observation weights vector observations × 1.
  • ic: The index of the column of ones that the estimator names with InterceptColumn, or nothing. The fit does not penalise or standardise that column.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
StatsAPI.fit(tgt::ElasticNet, X::MatNum, y::VecNum) -> ElasticNetFit

Fit an ElasticNet to the design X and the response y by cyclic coordinate descent.

ElasticNet states the objective. The fit solves it at one strength, which the strength rule gives. A design with few columns does not need the rest of the path as warm starts.

Algorithm

  1. Build the standardised design P, the weighted design Q and the scale s with elastic_net_design.
  2. Mark the penalised columns in pen: every column but tgt.ic. Compute nrm2, the weighted squared norm of each column.
  3. Start from the coefficients z at zero and the residual r equal to y. With an intercept column, fit that column alone first, and take its residual as r.
  4. Compute g, the product of each penalised column of Q with r, and the strength lam with penalty_strength.
  5. When every entry of g is zero, the start point is the optimum. Otherwise compute the threshold a and the ridge term ridge of each column, both zero at the intercept column, and run elastic_net_sweeps from the start point.
  6. Divide the coefficients by s, and return them with lam in an ElasticNetFit.

Arguments

  • tgt: The regression target.
  • X: The design matrix observations × regressors.
  • y: The response vector.

Returns

  • m::ElasticNetFit: The fitted model.
