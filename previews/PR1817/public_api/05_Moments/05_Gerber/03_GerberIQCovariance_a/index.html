Lowers the mixed-magnitude weights of a PartialGerberIQ template, so that the Gerber2 statistic stays inside [-1, 1]. It does not make the matrix positive definite. The pdm field of the estimator does that.

Under Gerber2, the entry of a pair is the net sum pos - neg, divided by the geometric mean of the two diagonal projections of the pair. A projection compares an asset with itself. So the projection is always concordant, and it gives a same-magnitude weight. The Internet Appendix of [36] states a condition for a bound of one. Each weight that joins two distinct magnitude classes must be at most the geometric mean of the two same-magnitude weights of those classes. The source proves that the condition is necessary and sufficient for its own alternative statistic, in Equations A1 to A9.

That statistic sums its diagonal projections over the observations on which both assets cross. The Gerber2 of the library sums them over the observations on which the one asset crosses, which is a larger set. So for the library, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality makes the condition sufficient. The condition stays necessary for the template as a whole, because a sample in which the two assets always cross together gives the denominator of the source. Six of the ten weights join two classes, and this method lowers each of them to its bound. With n1 = n4 = 0.2, an unclamped n7 = 1.0 gives 5.0, and the clamp makes it 1.0.

The four discordant weights need a case split. The template compares a concordant pair with dcp and dcn, and a discordant pair with ddp and ddn. So a return beyond a discordant boundary is not always beyond the concordant boundary that sets its diagonal class. When the two boundaries differ, the method uses the smaller of the two candidate weights. That bound holds for either class.

Note

The clamp is a necessary and sufficient condition on the template, and the statistic needs no other condition. sc needs no restriction, because Gerber2 computes its denominator in the units of the pair. The shipped defaults are exactly on the bound, so the clamp does not change them.

Mathematical definition

Let $h^{+}$ and $l^{+}$ be the diagonal weights of the two positive classes that a discordant channel can join. Let $h^{-}$ and $l^{-}$ be the diagonal weights of the two negative classes.

\[\begin{align} h^{+} &= \begin{cases} n_{4} & \delta_{d}^{+} \geq \delta_{c}^{+} \\ \min(n_{1}, n_{4}) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\,, &l^{+} &= \begin{cases} n_{1} & \delta_{d}^{+} \leq \delta_{c}^{+} \\ \min(n_{1}, n_{4}) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\,, \end{align}\]

The rule for $h^{-}$ and $l^{-}$ is the same, with $n_{5}$, $n_{2}$, $\delta_{d}^{-}$ and $\delta_{c}^{-}$. The six bounds are:

\[\begin{align} n_{7} &\leftarrow \min\left(n_{7},\, \sqrt{n_{1} n_{4}}\right)\,, & n_{8} &\leftarrow \min\left(n_{8},\, \sqrt{n_{2} n_{5}}\right)\,, \\ n_{6} &\leftarrow \min\left(n_{6},\, \sqrt{h^{+} h^{-}}\right)\,, & n_{9} &\leftarrow \min\left(n_{9},\, \sqrt{h^{+} l^{-}}\right)\,, \\ n_{10} &\leftarrow \min\left(n_{10},\, \sqrt{l^{+} h^{-}}\right)\,, & n_{3} &\leftarrow \min\left(n_{3},\, \sqrt{l^{+} l^{-}}\right)\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $n_{1}$, $n_{4}$: Small and large positive concordant weights.
  • $n_{2}$, $n_{5}$: Small and large negative concordant weights.
  • $\delta_{c}^{\pm}$, $\delta_{d}^{\pm}$: The concordant boundaries dcp and dcn, and the discordant boundaries ddp and ddn.

Algorithm

  1. Resolve the four discordant diagonal weights hip, lop, hin and lon from the two pairs of boundaries.
  2. Replace n7 and n8, the weights of the two mixed concordant channels, with their bounds when they are above them. Each bound is the geometric mean of the two weights that the channel joins.
  3. Replace n3, n6, n9 and n10, the weights of the four discordant channels, in the same way. Each bound uses the resolved weights that the channel joins.
  4. Return a new PartialGerberIQ with the six lowered weights and with each other field unchanged.

  • ::Gerber2: The Gerber2 marker.

Returns the delay e of the Gerber IQ decay. A rule in e computes the delay from X.

Mathematical definition

The default, nothing, computes the delay from the shape of the returns matrix alone.

\[\begin{align} \varepsilon &= \mathrm{round}\left(T - \frac{T}{N}\right)\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\varepsilon$: Delay. A co-movement of age $T - k$ keeps its full weight when $T - k \leq \varepsilon$.
  • $T$: Number of observations.
  • $N$: Number of assets.

The decay discounts an observation only when its index satisfies $k < T - \varepsilon$. So this default discounts about the oldest $T/N$ observations, and the other observations keep their full weight. The formula is the library's, not the formula of the source. The source states only that the delay is a whole number of periods, no larger than the lookback duration. It leaves the value to expert judgement or to an outer optimisation. Table 2 of [36] sets the delay to zero in the study of the source.

Algorithm

  1. Return the number unchanged when e is a Number.
  2. Call e(X) when e is a Function, and return its result.
  3. Read T and N from size(X) when e is nothing, and return round(Int, T - T / N).
  4. Throw an ArgumentError for a GerberIQEpsEstimator that implements no method of its own.

Arguments

  • e: The delay, or the rule that computes it.
    • ::Number: The method returns the number unchanged.
    • ::Function: A function that takes the data matrix X and returns a Number.
    • ::Nothing: The default. It returns round(Int, T - T / N), where T is the number of rows of X and N the number of columns.
    • ::GerberIQEpsEstimator: The fallback of the family, for a subtype that implements no method of its own. It always throws.
  • X: Data matrix observations × assets if the dims keyword does not exist or dims = 1, assets × observations when dims = 2.

Validation

  • A GerberIQEpsEstimator subtype that implements no method raises an ArgumentError that names the subtype. Without the refusal, such a subtype gets the default delay in silence.

Returns

  • e::Number: The delay of the decay. The decay does not discount an observation that is no older than e periods. The delay is not the window duration $\tau$ of the source, which the estimator does not expose. The estimator reads the whole matrix, and the decay discounts its oldest rows.

Computes the scaling factors of the thresholds of a pair of assets in the Gerber IQ statistic.

The statistic multiplies each threshold of the pair by the factor that this function returns for the axis of the threshold. The thresholds are the noise threshold c and each boundary of the squeezing template. So the scaler sets the units in which a return is large.

A scaler is pair-separable when its first factor uses sdi alone and its second factor uses sdj alone. The thresholds of an asset are then the same for every partner. AssetVolatilityGerberIQScaler is pair-separable. The default nothing is not, because the mean of the pair changes with sdj. A Function can be either. Every scaler is safe under every marker. Gerber2 computes its denominator in the units of the pair through iq_add_diagonal. So when a scaler changes the class of an asset, the numerator and the denominator change together.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} (s_i,\, s_j) &= \begin{cases} (\sigma_i,\, \sigma_j) & \text{AssetVolatilityGerberIQScaler} \\ \left(\dfrac{\sigma_i + \sigma_j}{2},\, \dfrac{\sigma_i + \sigma_j}{2}\right) & \text{nothing} \end{cases}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $s_i$, $s_j$: Threshold scaling factors of the pair.

struct BasicGerberIQ{__T_d, __T_n} <: GerberIQCovarianceAlgorithm

Squeezing template with one boundary and one weight for the whole return plane.

This template is the reduced template of the source. The results of the source use it, and it is the default of GerberIQCovariance.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \eta_{t,\,i,\,j} &= \begin{cases} 1 & d s_i \leq \lvert x_{t,\,i} \rvert \; \text{and} \; d s_j \leq \lvert x_{t,\,j} \rvert \\ n & \lvert x_{t,\,i} \rvert < d s_i \; \text{and} \; \lvert x_{t,\,j} \rvert < d s_j \\ n^2 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\eta_{t,\,i,\,j}$: Squeezing weight of the co-movement of assets $i$ and $j$ at observation $t$.
  • $x_{t,\,i}$: Return of asset $i$ at observation $t$.
  • $s_i$, $s_j$: Threshold scaling factors of the pair.
  • $d$: Significance threshold.
  • $n$: Squeezing weight.

The three cases are the tail, the body and the wing of the source. In the tail both returns are large, and in the body both returns are small. In the wing, one return is large and one return is small. Because $0 \leq n \leq 1$, the weights obey $n^2 \leq n \leq 1$. So a co-movement of two similar magnitudes counts no less than a co-movement of two different magnitudes. That order is the judgement of the source, and a template with one free weight cannot give a different order.

Section 3.3.5 of [36] gives the weights 1, η and η² to the tail, the body and the wing, with 0 ≤ η ≤ 1. Table 2 gives 2 to 3 as the typical range of the boundary. The defaults d = 2 and n = 0.5 are the choice of the library in those ranges. The figures of the source show no weight on the branches of the noise zone, where one asset is inside the noise threshold. Section 2 states only that a method can use such data or not. The library gives a co-movement there the weight of the channel that the same rule selects, as the diagram shows.

The body case happens only when $c < d$. The statistic drops a co-movement with both returns inside the noise threshold before the template. So at $c = d$ the band of n is empty, and only $1$ and $n^2$ occur.

Estimates a covariance matrix from the weighted and age-discounted co-movements of each pair of assets.

Four fields hold the parameters of the source. c is the noise threshold, and kind is the squeezing template that gives the weight of a co-movement. sc sets the units of the thresholds, and decay discounts a co-movement by its age. The source has no parameter for alg, because its statistic is the Gerber1 statistic alone. This estimator also gives Gerber0 and Gerber2 from the classic Gerber family. gerber_IQ states the three statistics and their reduction to the Gerber family.

The lookback duration $\tau$ of the source has no field. The estimator always reads each row of X, and decay discounts the oldest rows. This is the case $\tau = T - 1$ of the source.

Gerber1 is Equation 4 of [36]. Its numerator sums the signed, weighted and discounted contributions over the observations on which both assets leave the noise zone. Its denominator sums the weights over the observations on which at least one asset leaves it. Equation 5 is the discount. A direct evaluation of Equations 4 and 5 for BasicGerberIQ agrees with this estimator to machine precision.

The field oc states the effective number of observations of the matrix. The library adds it, and the source has no such parameter. A denoiser in a PortfolioOptimisersCovariance reads the ratio T / N, and it gets T from effective_observation_count. The decay gives the old rows less weight, so the matrix is worth fewer observations than X has rows. But the statistic is a ratio of weighted counts of co-movements over thresholds, and not a weighted moment, so no count follows from it. oc = nothing keeps T = size(X, 1). KishObservationCount gives the Kish count of the temporal weights decay(T, k), $k = 1, \ldots, T$.

The library takes Gerber0 and Gerber2 from the classic Gerber family. The Internet Appendix of the source states a third form, which the library does not ship. Equation 1 of the source drops a return with $\lvert r^{*} \rvert \leq c$, so a return exactly at the threshold is noise there. The library lets that return cross, which is the closed rule of the Gerber statistic. The source centres the returns on a location $r_0$ that the user chooses. The library centres them on the mean that me computes.
