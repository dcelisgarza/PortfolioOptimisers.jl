PortfolioOptimisers.homogeneity_degree — Function
homogeneity_degree(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, fees::Option{<:Fees} = nothing,
                   T::Option{<:Integer} = nothing) -> Option{<:Integer}

Return the degree of homogeneity of the risk measure r in the portfolio weights, under the fees fees on T observations, or nothing when r has no degree.

adjust_risk_contribution divides a risk by this degree, so that the risk contributions of risk_contribution sum to the risk. A measure that no other method names has degree 1, unless it measures a distance to a fixed target that is not zero. has_fixed_target finds such a target, and the measure then has no degree, because $|x - c|$ with a fixed $c \neq 0$ is not homogeneous in $x$.

The degree is a property of the risk as a function of the weights, so it reads the fees that the risk pays. A fee that does not scale with the weights is a fixed target on the net returns. A turnover fee against previous weights that are not zero, a liquidation charge and a fixed fee are such fees, and has_fixed_fee finds them. A measure that reads the net returns then has no degree, unless the fee cancels from its risk. A proportional fee, and a turnover fee against zero weights, scale with the weights and keep the degree.

Whether a fee cancels from the risk of an OWA measure depends on the sum of its weights, and a weight builder gives the weights of the observation count. adjusted_risk passes the count T of its returns matrix. Without T, the sum of a builder is not known, and the fee does not cancel.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} R(c \boldsymbol{w}) &= c^{d} R(\boldsymbol{w}) \quad \forall c > 0\,, \\ d\left(\frac{R_1}{R_2}\right) &= d(R_1) - d(R_2)\,. \end{align}\]

The second line gives the degree of a ratio of two homogeneous measures. A scalarised vector of measures has the degree of its elements when every element has the same degree, and the sum, the maximum and the minimum keep that degree.

Where:

  • $R(\boldsymbol{w})$: Portfolio risk.
  • $\boldsymbol{w}$: Portfolio weights vector $N \times 1$.
  • $d$: Degree of homogeneity of the risk measure, the $d$ for which $R(c \boldsymbol{w}) = c^{d} R(\boldsymbol{w})$ at every $c > 0$.
  • $R_1$, $R_2$: The risk measures of the numerator and of the denominator of a ratio.

Algorithm

  1. Return nothing when has_fixed_target of r, fees and T is true. This applies to steps 2 and 7.
  2. Return 2 for a member of SquaredRiskMeasures, 3 for a member of CubedRiskMeasures, 4 for a member of FourthPowerRiskMeasures, and 0 for a member of ScaleFreeRiskMeasures.
  3. For RiskTrackingRiskMeasure, return nothing when its benchmark is a fixed target. Otherwise return the degree of its measure r under fees and T. The benchmark weights are zero at this step, so the measure tracks r itself on the same net returns.
  4. For RiskRatio and NonOptimisationRiskRatio, get the degree of each side under fees and T with scalarised_degree, and subtract the degree of r2 from the degree of r1 with degree_difference.
  5. For MeanReturnRiskRatio and ExpectedReturnRiskRatio, return nothing when rf is not zero. Otherwise subtract the degree of rk from the degree of rt, in the same way. A return estimator does not read T. ExpectedReturn has the degree of its return estimator rt.
  6. Return nothing for VarianceSkewKurtosis, whose three terms have the degrees 2, 3 and 4, and for a member of RelativeDrawdownRiskMeasures.
  7. Return 1 for every other measure.

Arguments

  • r: Risk measure or vector of risk measures.
  • fees: The fees that the risk pays, or nothing.
  • T: The number of observations of the net returns, or nothing when it is not known.

Returns

  • d::Option{<:Integer}: The degree of homogeneity, or nothing when r is not homogeneous.

has_fixed_target(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure) -> Bool
has_fixed_target(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, fees::Option{<:Fees},
                 T::Option{<:Integer} = nothing) -> Bool
has_fixed_target(target) -> Bool

Return true when the risk measure r measures a distance to a target that is not zero and does not scale with the portfolio weights.

Such a measure is not homogeneous in the weights, so homogeneity_degree gives it no degree. A target that scales with the weights, such as the expected portfolio return $\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\intercal} \boldsymbol{w}$, keeps the degree of the measure. The second form also reads the fees: a fee that does not scale with the weights moves the net returns by a fixed amount, so it is a fixed target of a measure that reads the net returns. The third form reads one target value. A Turnover fee is a distance to its previous weights.

Algorithm

  1. For a LowOrderMoment, a HighOrderMoment, a ThirdCentralMoment and a MedianAbsoluteDeviation, read the Moment Target mu. A number is fixed when it is not zero. A VecScalar is fixed when its offset s is not zero.
  2. For a TrackingRiskMeasure and a RiskTrackingRiskMeasure, read the benchmark tr. The weights of a WeightsTracking and the series of a ReturnsTracking are fixed when they are not zero. A vector B of a DataBenchmarkTracking is fixed when it is not zero, and so is an unbound B, whose target is not known. A matrix B gives the series $\mathbf{B} \boldsymbol{w}$, which scales with the weights.
  3. For a TurnoverRiskMeasure, the reference weights w are fixed when they are not zero.
  4. For a Turnover fee, the previous weights w are fixed when an asset whose rate is not zero has a previous weight that is not zero.
  5. With fees, return true when step 1, 2 or 3 is true. Otherwise return true when r reads the fees, reads_fees, and has_fixed_fee finds a fee that does not cancel from the risk of r on T observations, cancels_constant_fee.
  6. Return false for every other measure and every other target value.

Arguments

  • r: Risk measure or vector of risk measures.
  • fees: The fees that the risk pays, or nothing.
  • T: The number of observations of the net returns, or nothing when it is not known.
  • target: The target value of a measure.

Returns

  • flag::Bool: true when the target is fixed and not zero.

PortfolioOptimisers.has_fixed_fee — Function
has_fixed_fee(fees::Option{<:Fees}, cancels::Bool) -> Bool

Return true when fees holds a fee that does not scale with the portfolio weights and that does not cancel from the risk of a measure.

A periodic fee falls on each observation. The term of a Turnover fee whose previous weights are not zero is such a fee, and so is the proportional liquidation charge lq, which does not read the weights at all. A one-off fee is a fixed fee fl or fs, or the fixed liquidation charge flq. It counts the positions, so it does not scale with the weights. The Fee Amortisation fa names its clock. AmortisedFees spreads it over each observation, so it is then a periodic fee. Otherwise it falls on the first observation alone.

A periodic fee moves each net return by the same amount, so it cancels from the risk of a measure that a constant shift does not change. cancels states that property of the measure, from cancels_constant_fee. A one-off fee on the first observation moves one return, and it cancels from no measure. A proportional fee l or s, and a turnover fee against zero weights, scale with the weights, so they are not fixed.

Algorithm

  1. Return false for a nothing fees.
  2. Get the flags periodic and one_off on the clock fees.fa with fixed_fee_terms.
  3. Return true when one_off is true, or when periodic is true and cancels is false.

Arguments

  • fees: The fees that the risk pays, or nothing.
  • cancels::Bool: Whether a fee that every observation pays in the same amount cancels from the risk.

Returns

  • flag::Bool: true when a fee that does not scale with the weights reaches the risk.

PortfolioOptimisers.adjust_risk_contribution — Function
adjust_risk_contribution(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, val::Number,
                         fees::Option{<:Fees} = nothing,
                         T::Option{<:Integer} = nothing) -> Number

Divide the risk val of the measure r by the degree of homogeneity of r under the fees fees on T observations.

By Euler's theorem, the gradient of the divided risk, multiplied by the weights, sums to the risk. adjusted_risk calls this function, and risk_contribution differentiates the result. A measure of degree 0, and a measure with no degree, keep val unchanged.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} A(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \frac{R(\boldsymbol{w})}{d}\,, \\ \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \frac{\partial A(\boldsymbol{w})}{\partial w_i} &= R(\boldsymbol{w})\,. \end{align}\]

The second line holds when $R$ is homogeneous of a degree $d \neq 0$. At $d = 0$ the sum is zero for every $R$, so no division gives back the risk, and the contributions of a measure of ScaleFreeRiskMeasures sum to zero. A measure that is not homogeneous, such as a tracking error or a turnover, has contributions that do not sum to its risk in general.

Where:

  • $A(\boldsymbol{w})$: Homogeneity-adjusted risk.
  • $R(\boldsymbol{w})$: Portfolio risk.
  • $d$: Degree of homogeneity of the risk measure, the $d$ for which $R(c \boldsymbol{w}) = c^{d} R(\boldsymbol{w})$ at every $c > 0$.
  • $w_i$: Weight of asset $i$.
  • $N$: Number of assets.

Arguments

  • r: Risk measure or vector of risk measures.
  • val::Number: Risk of r at the weights.
  • fees: The fees that the risk pays, or nothing.
  • T: The number of observations of the net returns, or nothing when it is not known.

Returns

  • Number: val / d, or val when the degree d is 0 or nothing.

Examples

julia> PortfolioOptimisers.adjust_risk_contribution(Variance(), 0.5)0.25julia> PortfolioOptimisers.adjust_risk_contribution(EqualRisk(), 0.5)0.5
