PortfolioOptimisers.worst_loss_distribution — Function
worst_loss_distribution(x::VecNum, ::Nothing) -> Vector
worst_loss_distribution(x::VecNum, w::VecNum) -> Vector

Return the Worst-Case Distribution of a measure whose value is the largest loss among the observations of positive weight.

The distribution puts all its mass on the observations at the loss of worst_positive_weight_loss, in proportion to their weights. ERM_worst_case reads it at alpha = 0, and PRM_worst_case reads it under the condition of PowerNormValueatRisk.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} L^{*} &= \underset{t \in \mathcal{I}}{\max} \; (-x_t)\,, \\ q_t &= \frac{w_t \, \mathbb{1}\{-x_t = L^{*}\}}{\sum_{s \in \mathcal{I}} w_s \, \mathbb{1}\{-x_s = L^{*}\}}\,, \quad t \in \mathcal{I}\,, \\ q_t &= 0\,, \quad t \notin \mathcal{I}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $L^{*}$: Largest loss among the observations of positive weight.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}$: Worst-Case Distribution $T \times 1$, one probability for each observation. Its entries are not negative, and they sum to one.
  • $\boldsymbol{x}$: Portfolio returns vector $T \times 1$.
  • $w_{t}$: Observation weight of observation $t$. Without observation weights, every $w_t$ is one.
  • $\mathcal{I}$: Set of the observations of positive weight. Without observation weights, it holds all $T$ observations.
  • $\mathbb{1}\{\cdot\}$: Indicator, one when the condition holds and zero otherwise.

The expected loss under $\boldsymbol{q}$ is $L^{*}$, so $\boldsymbol{q}$ reaches the largest loss. When two or more observations tie at $L^{*}$, every distribution on the tied observations reaches it, and the measure has no gradient. By Theorem 17 of [300], each such distribution gives a subgradient of a coherent measure. The function gives the subgradient that shares the mass by weight.

Arguments

  • x: Return or drawdown series. A loss is a negative entry. The function does not change it.
  • w: Resolved observation weights from checked_observation_weights, or nothing.

Returns

  • q::Vector: The distribution, one entry for each observation. Its entries sum to one.

PortfolioOptimisers.worst_case_distribution — Function
worst_case_distribution(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, x::VecNum)
worst_case_distribution(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, w::VecNum, X::MatNum,
                        fees::Option{<:Fees})

Compute the Worst-Case Distribution of a measure whose value is a solve, or return nothing for every other measure.

The measures of the entropic, the relativistic and the power norm families solve a conic programme for their value. linearised_risk evaluates the expected loss under the distribution that this function returns, and risk_contribution and risk_gradient take the finite difference of that expected loss. A finite difference of the measure itself differences two solves, and the noise of the solver is as large as the change that the step makes.

The method on a return series is the one that a measure implements. Each measure of the three families reads its distribution with ERM_worst_case, RRM_worst_case or PRM_worst_case, on the series that its functor reads. A range measure returns the pair of the distributions of its loss tail and its gain tail, through worst_case_pair.

A composite that wraps other measures returns a tuple of their distributions. These composites are a vector of measures, RiskRatio, NonOptimisationRiskRatio, MeanReturnRiskRatio, RiskTrackingRiskMeasure and GenericValueatRiskRange. The tuple of the dependent tracking measure also holds the risk of the benchmark, which does not move with the weights. A tail of a GenericValueatRiskRange whose value is not a solve has nothing in the tuple. linearised_risk applies the formula of the composite to the linearised values of the measures that it wraps.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \boldsymbol{x} &= \mathbf{X} \boldsymbol{w} - F(\boldsymbol{w})\,, \\ R(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \underset{\boldsymbol{q} \in \mathcal{Q}}{\max} \; -\boldsymbol{q}^\intercal \boldsymbol{y}(\boldsymbol{x})\,, \\ \nabla_{\boldsymbol{w}} R(\boldsymbol{w}) &= -\mathbf{J}^\intercal \boldsymbol{q}^{*}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{x}$: Portfolio returns vector $T \times 1$.
  • $\mathbf{X}$: Returns matrix $T \times N$.
  • $\boldsymbol{w}$: Portfolio weights vector $N \times 1$.
  • $F(\boldsymbol{w})$: Fee series $T \times 1$ of the portfolio, zero when fees is nothing. See calc_net_returns.
  • $R(\boldsymbol{w})$: Portfolio risk.
  • $\mathcal{Q}$: Set of distributions of the measure. It is a Kullback-Leibler ball for the entropic family and a Kaniadakis ball for the relativistic family.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}$: Worst-Case Distribution $T \times 1$, one probability for each observation. Its entries are not negative, and they sum to one.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}^{*}$: Maximiser of the expected loss over $\mathcal{Q}$, the Worst-Case Distribution that the function returns.
  • $\boldsymbol{y}(\boldsymbol{x})$: Measured series $T \times 1$, the series that the risk measure reads. It is the returns, the absolute drawdowns or the relative drawdowns.
  • $\mathbf{J}$: Jacobian of $\boldsymbol{y}(\boldsymbol{x})$ with respect to $\boldsymbol{w}$, $T \times N$. For a measure of the returns without fees, it is $\mathbf{X}$.

The measures of the three families are coherent. Proposition 4.1 of [191] states that a coherent measure is the largest expected loss over a set of probability measures. That is the second line. Proposition 5 of [300] applies when the maximiser is unique. It states that the derivative of a coherent measure in a direction is then the expected loss of that direction under the maximiser. The chain rule through $\boldsymbol{y}$ and $\boldsymbol{x}$ then gives the third line, where $\boldsymbol{y}$ is differentiable. When the maximiser is not unique, the measure of $\boldsymbol{y}$ has no gradient. Theorem 17 of [300] states that each maximiser then gives a subgradient.

Algorithm

The method on the weights does these steps:

  1. Check the slots of r with assert_resolved_slots and assert_calibrated_slots, as expected_risk does.
  2. Read the kind of r with risk_input_kind. For a kind other than NetReturnsInput, return nothing.
  3. Compute the net returns x of w with calc_net_returns.
  4. Return the method of r on x.

A composite replaces these steps with its own method on the weights, which calls this function on each measure that it wraps.

Arguments

  • r: Risk measure or vector of risk measures.
  • x::VecNum: Net return series of the portfolio.
  • w::VecNum: Portfolio weights vector assets × 1.
  • X::MatNum: Returns matrix observations × assets.
  • fees: Optional Fees structure.

Validation

  • The method on the weights raises through assert_resolved_slots and assert_calibrated_slots, as expected_risk does.

Returns

  • q::VecNum: The Worst-Case Distribution, one entry for each observation. When the maximiser is not unique, it is the maximiser that the solver finds.
  • (q_loss, q_gain): The distributions of the two tails of a range measure.
  • Tuple: The distributions of the measures that a composite wraps.
  • Vector: The distribution of each element of a vector of measures.
  • nothing: For a measure whose value is not a solve, and when no solver solves the programme or gives its dual.

PortfolioOptimisers.worst_case_risk — Function
worst_case_risk(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, x::VecNum, q::VecNum) -> Number
worst_case_risk(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, x::VecNum, q::Tuple) -> Number

Evaluate the expected loss of the series that r reads from the net returns x, under the fixed Worst-Case Distribution q.

A measure on the returns reads x itself. A drawdown measure implements a method that reads its drawdown series instead. A range measure carries a pair, and the gain tail is the loss of the negated series. GenericValueatRiskRange implements a method that adds its two tails through tail_worst_case_risk.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \tilde{R}(\boldsymbol{x}) &= -\boldsymbol{q}^\intercal \boldsymbol{y}(\boldsymbol{x})\,, \\ \tilde{R}_{\mathrm{range}}(\boldsymbol{x}) &= -\boldsymbol{q}_{L}^\intercal \boldsymbol{x} + \boldsymbol{q}_{G}^\intercal \boldsymbol{x}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\tilde{R}$: Linearised risk, the expected loss of the measured series under a fixed Worst-Case Distribution.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}$: Worst-Case Distribution $T \times 1$, one probability for each observation. Its entries are not negative, and they sum to one.
  • $\boldsymbol{y}(\boldsymbol{x})$: Measured series $T \times 1$, the series that the risk measure reads. It is the returns, the absolute drawdowns or the relative drawdowns.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}_{L}$, $\boldsymbol{q}_{G}$: Worst-Case Distributions of the loss tail and of the gain tail of a range measure.
  • $\boldsymbol{x}$: Portfolio returns vector $T \times 1$.

At the series that gave $\boldsymbol{q}$, $\tilde{R}$ equals the value of the measure, to the tolerance of the solver. Its gradient with respect to $\boldsymbol{x}$ is the gradient of the measure, as worst_case_distribution states.

Arguments

  • r: Risk measure or vector of risk measures.
  • x::VecNum: Net return series of the portfolio.
  • q: Worst-Case Distribution, or the pair of a range measure.

Returns

  • rk::Number: The expected loss under q.

PortfolioOptimisers.linearised_risk — Function
linearised_risk(q::Nothing, r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, w::VecNum, X::MatNum,
                fees::Option{<:Fees}; kwargs...)
linearised_risk(q, r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, w::VecNum, X::MatNum,
                fees::Option{<:Fees}; kwargs...)
linearised_risk(qs::AbstractVector, rs::VecBaseRM, w::VecNum, X::MatNum,
                fees::Option{<:Fees}; sca::Scalariser = SumScalariser(), kwargs...)

Evaluate the risk of r at w, or its expected loss under the fixed Worst-Case Distribution q, for a finite difference.

Without a distribution, the function returns expected_risk. With one, it returns worst_case_risk of the net returns at w. Both have the same gradient at the weights that gave q, but the second differences no solve.

A composite, whose q is a tuple from worst_case_distribution, applies its own formula to the linearised values of the measures that it wraps, as its expected_risk method does to their values. A vector of measures scalarises them with sca, and the tracking measure with independent variables reads the series of difference_risk through difference_linearised_risk.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \tilde{R}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= -\boldsymbol{q}^\intercal \boldsymbol{y}(\boldsymbol{x}(\boldsymbol{w}))\,, \\ \tilde{R}(\boldsymbol{w}_0) &= R(\boldsymbol{w}_0)\,, \\ \nabla_{\boldsymbol{w}} \tilde{R}(\boldsymbol{w}_0) &= \nabla_{\boldsymbol{w}} R(\boldsymbol{w}_0)\,. \end{align}\]

The composites apply their formulas to the linearised values of the measures that they wrap:

\[\begin{align} \tilde{R}_{\mathrm{vec}}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= S\left(s_1 \tilde{R}_1(\boldsymbol{w}), \ldots, s_K \tilde{R}_K(\boldsymbol{w})\right)\,, \\ \tilde{R}_{\mathrm{ratio}}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \frac{\tilde{R}_1(\boldsymbol{w})}{\tilde{R}_2(\boldsymbol{w})}\,, \\ \tilde{R}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \frac{m(\boldsymbol{w}) - r_f}{\tilde{R}_1(\boldsymbol{w})}\,, \\ \tilde{R}_{\mathrm{dep}}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \left| \tilde{R}_1(\boldsymbol{w}) - R_1(\boldsymbol{w}_b) \right|\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\tilde{R}$: Linearised risk, the expected loss of the measured series under a fixed Worst-Case Distribution.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}$: Worst-Case Distribution $T \times 1$, one probability for each observation. Its entries are not negative, and they sum to one.
  • $\boldsymbol{y}(\boldsymbol{x})$: Measured series $T \times 1$, the series that the risk measure reads. It is the returns, the absolute drawdowns or the relative drawdowns.
  • $\boldsymbol{x}(\boldsymbol{w})$: Net return series of the portfolio at $\boldsymbol{w}$.
  • $\boldsymbol{w}$: Portfolio weights vector $N \times 1$.
  • $\boldsymbol{w}_0$: Portfolio weights at which the solve gave $\boldsymbol{q}$.
  • $R(\boldsymbol{w})$: Portfolio risk.
  • $\tilde{R}_k$, $R_k$: Linearised risk and risk of the $k$-th measure that the composite wraps. Without a distribution, $\tilde{R}_k = R_k$.
  • $S$: Scalariser that combines the scaled risks of a vector of risk measures into one number.
  • $s_k$: scale of the $k$-th risk measure of a vector, its weight in the combination.
  • $K$: Number of measures in the vector.
  • $m(\boldsymbol{w})$: Mean net return of the portfolio, the value of the return measure of MeanReturnRiskRatio.
  • $r_f$: Risk-free rate, the rf field of MeanReturnRiskRatio.
  • $\boldsymbol{w}_b$: Benchmark portfolio weights vector $N \times 1$, the w of the tracking specification tr.

The first line is linear in $\boldsymbol{y}$, so its finite difference holds no solver noise. The second and the third lines hold to the tolerance of the solver when $\boldsymbol{q}$ is the unique maximiser, as worst_case_distribution states. By the chain rule, each composite formula then has the gradient of the composite at $\boldsymbol{w}_0$. The ratios cover RiskRatio and NonOptimisationRiskRatio, and $\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{dep}}$ is RiskTrackingRiskMeasure with DependentVariableTracking. With IndependentVariableTracking, the tracking measure is $\tilde{R}_1$ of the active weights.

Arguments

  • q: Worst-Case Distribution from worst_case_distribution, or nothing.
  • qs: Worst-Case Distribution of each measure of rs, or nothing for a measure without one.
  • r: Risk measure or vector of risk measures.
  • rs: Vector of risk measures.
  • w::VecNum: Portfolio weights vector assets × 1.
  • X::MatNum: Returns matrix observations × assets.
  • fees: Optional Fees structure.
  • sca: Scalariser for combining multiple risk measures.

Returns

  • rk::Number: The risk, or the expected loss under q.

PortfolioOptimisers.difference_worst_case — Function
difference_worst_case(r::AbstractBaseRiskMeasure, wd::VecNum, w::VecNum, X::MatNum,
                      fees::Option{<:Fees})

Compute the Worst-Case Distribution of r on the active weights wd, on the series that difference_risk reads.

RiskTrackingRiskMeasure with IndependentVariableTracking measures the risk of the active weights, and charges the fees on the portfolio weights w. Without fees, the series is the net return series of wd, so the function reads the distribution on the weights. With fees, it reads the distribution of a measure of the kind NetReturnsInput on the series that difference_risk builds, and it returns nothing for every other kind.

With fees, difference_risk decomposes a RiskRatio and a nested RiskTrackingRiskMeasure into the measures that they wrap. So the function returns a tuple of the distributions of those measures, as worst_case_distribution does without fees. The tuple of the dependent tracking measure also holds the risk of its benchmark.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \boldsymbol{w}_d &= \boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{w}_b\,, \\ \boldsymbol{x}_d &= \mathbf{X} \boldsymbol{w}_d - F(\boldsymbol{w})\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{w}_d$: Active weights vector $N \times 1$.
  • $\boldsymbol{w}$: Portfolio weights vector $N \times 1$.
  • $\boldsymbol{w}_b$: Benchmark portfolio weights vector $N \times 1$, the w of the tracking specification tr.
  • $\boldsymbol{x}_d$: Series of the active weights $T \times 1$, the series that difference_risk measures.
  • $\mathbf{X}$: Returns matrix $T \times N$.
  • $F(\boldsymbol{w})$: Fee series $T \times 1$ of the portfolio, zero when fees is nothing. See calc_net_returns.

The function returns the Worst-Case Distribution of r on $\boldsymbol{x}_d$.

Arguments

  • r: Risk measure or vector of risk measures.
  • wd::VecNum: Active weights, the portfolio weights minus the benchmark weights.
  • w::VecNum: Portfolio weights vector assets × 1.
  • X::MatNum: Returns matrix observations × assets.
  • fees: Optional Fees structure.

Validation

  • With fees, the function raises through assert_resolved_slots and assert_calibrated_slots, as the functor of the tracking measure does.

Returns

  • The value of worst_case_distribution on the series of the active weights.
  • Tuple: With fees, the distributions of the measures that a ratio or a nested tracking measure wraps.
