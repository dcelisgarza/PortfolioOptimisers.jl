PortfolioOptimisers.PSEUDO_R2_VARIANTS — Constant
PSEUDO_R2_VARIANTS

Tuple of the pseudo-$R^2$ variants StatsAPI.r2 accepts for a fitted GeneralisedLinearModel.

The members are :McFadden, :CoxSnell, :Nagelkerke and :devianceratio. The constructor of GeneralisedLinearModel checks its variant field against this tuple. A generalised linear model has no classical $R^2$, so each member scores the fitted model against the intercept-only model of the same family instead.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} R^2_{\mathrm{McF}} &= 1 - \frac{\ln\hat{L}}{\ln\hat{L}_{0}}\,,\\ R^2_{\mathrm{CS}} &= 1 - \left(\frac{\hat{L}_{0}}{\hat{L}}\right)^{2/T}\,,\\ R^2_{\mathrm{N}} &= \frac{R^2_{\mathrm{CS}}}{1 - \hat{L}_{0}^{\,2/T}}\,,\\ R^2_{\mathrm{dev}} &= 1 - \frac{D}{D_{0}}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $R^2_{\mathrm{McF}}$: :McFadden.
  • $R^2_{\mathrm{CS}}$: :CoxSnell.
  • $R^2_{\mathrm{N}}$: :Nagelkerke.
  • $R^2_{\mathrm{dev}}$: :devianceratio.
  • $\hat{L}$: Maximum likelihood of the fitted model.
  • $\hat{L}_{0}$: Maximum likelihood of the intercept-only model of the same family.
  • $D$: Deviance of the fitted model.
  • $D_{0}$: Deviance of the intercept-only model of the same family.
  • $T$: Number of observations.

The Normal family is the default of GeneralisedLinearModel, and three facts hold for it. Its deviance is the residual sum of squares, so $R^2_{\mathrm{dev}}$ is the classical $R^2$ of the same fit. Its maximum likelihood carries the fitted dispersion, so $\left(\hat{L}_{0}/\hat{L}\right)^{2/T} = D/D_{0}$ and $R^2_{\mathrm{CS}}$ equals $R^2_{\mathrm{dev}}$.

Its likelihood is a density and not a probability, so $\hat{L}_{0}$ can be above or below one. The two forms on the log-likelihood, $R^2_{\mathrm{McF}}$ and $R^2_{\mathrm{N}}$, can then leave $[0, 1]$ in either direction. A change of the scale of the response alone moves both from above one to below zero, and $R^2_{\mathrm{dev}}$ does not change. Only $R^2_{\mathrm{dev}}$ is continuous with the LinearModel path, so default_regression_criterion_variant returns :devianceratio.

ADJUSTED_PSEUDO_R2_VARIANTS

Tuple of the pseudo-$R^2$ variants StatsAPI.adjr2 accepts for a fitted GeneralisedLinearModel.

The members are :McFadden and :devianceratio, a strict subset of PSEUDO_R2_VARIANTS. GeneralisedLinearModel cannot check its variant against this tuple, because it does not know which criterion reads the variant. StepwiseRegression checks it, because it is the first type that holds the criterion and the target together. StatsAPI.adjr2 raises an ArgumentError on each variant that this tuple omits.

Mathematical definition

Each member discounts its unadjusted form of PSEUDO_R2_VARIANTS by the number of parameters of the model. [282] states the unadjusted form alone, and the discount of each member is the convention of the GLM package. This docstring cites no original source for the discount.

\[\begin{align} \bar{R}^2_{\mathrm{McF}} &= 1 - \frac{\ln\hat{L} - k}{\ln\hat{L}_{0}}\,,\\ \bar{R}^2_{\mathrm{dev}} &= 1 - \frac{D\,(T - 1)}{D_{0}\,(T - k)}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\bar{R}^2_{\mathrm{McF}}$: :McFadden.
  • $\bar{R}^2_{\mathrm{dev}}$: :devianceratio.
  • $\hat{L}$: Maximum likelihood of the fitted model.
  • $\hat{L}_{0}$: Maximum likelihood of the intercept-only model of the same family.
  • $D$: Deviance of the fitted model.
  • $D_{0}$: Deviance of the intercept-only model of the same family.
  • $k$: Number of estimated parameters, which is StatsAPI.dof of the fitted model. It counts the regression coefficients, the intercept, and the dispersion when the family estimates one. The Normal family estimates it, and the binomial and Poisson families do not.
  • $T$: Number of observations.

This $k$ is the count that :aic, :aicc and :bic read. It is not the predictor count that :adjr2 reads on a fitted LinearModel. STEPWISE_REGRESSION_CRITERIA states both counts.

PortfolioOptimisers.STEPWISE_REGRESSION_CRITERIA — Constant
STEPWISE_REGRESSION_CRITERIA

Tuple of the symbols that name the stepwise regression criteria with one score for a fitted model.

StepwiseRegression accepts each symbol of this tuple in its crit field, and stores it as a Val. regression_criterion_func, regression_polarity, regression_threshold and the get_*_reg_incl*! helpers dispatch on that Val. The constructor rejects a symbol outside the tuple.

PValue is not a member. It reads the p-values of the coefficients of the fitted model, and not one score, so it is a type with its own stepwise methods. :aic, :aicc and :bic score a fitted LinearModel and a fitted GeneralisedLinearModel the same way. GLM defines :r2 and :adjr2 for a fitted LinearModel only, so under the other target they read a named pseudo-$R^2$ variant.

Mathematical definition

:aic, the Akaike information criterion

The criterion trades the fitted likelihood against the number of estimated parameters. A lower value is a better model.

\[\begin{align} \mathrm{AIC} &= 2k - 2\ln\hat{L}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $k$: Number of estimated parameters, which is StatsAPI.dof of the fitted model. It counts the regression coefficients, the intercept and the residual variance.
  • $\hat{L}$: Maximum likelihood of the model.

:aicc, the Akaike information criterion with the small-sample correction

The correction term grows as $T$ comes near $k$. On a short sample, :aicc then gives a large model a larger penalty than :aic does.

\[\begin{align} \mathrm{AICC} &= \mathrm{AIC} + \frac{2k(k+1)}{T - k - 1}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $k$: Number of estimated parameters, as for :aic.
  • $T$: Number of observations.

The correction term divides by $T - k - 1$. The criterion has no value when $T = k + 1$, and the term changes sign below it. So :aicc needs a sample longer than the largest model that the search can reach.

:bic, the Bayesian information criterion

The penalty is $k \ln T$ and not $2k$. It is larger than the penalty of :aic for $T \geq 8$, so the search usually stops with fewer factors. [285] maximises the log-likelihood less $\tfrac{1}{2} k \ln T$. The form below multiplies that score by minus two, which keeps the order of the models and makes a lower value better.

\[\begin{align} \mathrm{BIC} &= k\ln T - 2\ln\hat{L}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $k$: Number of estimated parameters, as for :aic.
  • $T$: Number of observations.
  • $\hat{L}$: Maximum likelihood of the model.

:r2, the coefficient of determination

The criterion is the share of the variance of the response that the model explains. A higher value is a better fit.

\[\begin{align} R^2 &= 1 - \frac{\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}}{\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}} = 1 - \frac{\sum_t (y_t - \hat{y}_t)^2}{\sum_t (y_t - \bar{y})^2}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}$: Residual sum of squares.
  • $\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}$: Total sum of squares.
  • $y_t$: Observed response at time $t$.
  • $\hat{y}_t$: Fitted response at time $t$.
  • $\bar{y}$: Mean of the observed responses.

$R^2$ never falls when the model gets one more factor, so it gives no penalty for the size of the model. Under ForwardSelection it admits every factor, and under BackwardElimination it removes no factor. Use :adjr2, :aic, :aicc or :bic when the criterion must penalise the size of the model.

:adjr2, the adjusted coefficient of determination

The adjustment discounts $R^2$ by the degrees of freedom of the predictors. Unlike the score of :r2, the score of :adjr2 can fall when the model gets one more factor, and the search can stop on it.

\[\begin{align} \bar{R}^2 &= 1 - (1 - R^2) \frac{T - 1}{T - k - 1}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $k$: Number of predictors, without the intercept. :aic, :aicc and :bic use $k$ for a different count, which adds the intercept and the residual variance to the predictors.
  • $T$: Number of observations.

The method dispatches on the Val that names the criterion and on the regression target, because the two maximisation criteria read a different quantity under each target. The map is:

:aiccStatsAPI.aiccStatsAPI.aicc

PortfolioOptimisers.regression_polarity — Function
regression_polarity(crit::MinMaxValStepwiseRegressionCriterion)

Return the three functions that state which direction of a stepwise criterion is better.

A stepwise search asks the same three questions of every criterion: which entry of a score vector is the best one, whether a candidate score improves on the score in hand, and what the worst score of a type is. Each answer is one function under a minimised criterion and its opposite under a maximised one. This method pair is the only place in the library that states the pairing. regression_threshold, get_forward_reg_incl_excl!, get_backward_reg_incl! and the two _regression methods that fill a score vector all read it, and none of them states the pairing again.

Arguments

  • crit: The criterion, as the Val that the crit field of StepwiseRegression holds.

Returns

  • polarity::NamedTuple: Three functions.

    • best: findmin under a minimised criterion, findmax under a maximised one. Returns the best entry of a score vector and its index.
    • improves: < under a minimised criterion, > under a maximised one. Returns whether the first score is better than the second.
    • worst: typemax under a minimised criterion, typemin under a maximised one. Returns the worst score of the type it is given.

PortfolioOptimisers.glm_weights — Function
glm_weights(w::StatsBase.AbstractWeights) -> StatsBase.FrequencyWeights

Return the frequency weights that GLM reads as the observation weights w.

GLM reads every weight vector as frequency weights, so the number of observations of a weighted fit is $\sum_t w_t$. StatsBase.FrequencyWeights state a count, so the method returns them unchanged. Every other kind of weights states the relative weight of each observation and no count. For example, GLM reads probability weights that sum to one as one observation. The fit then has a residual degree of freedom at or below zero, and a p-value of one for every coefficient. To prevent this, the method scales the other kinds to sum to their Kish effective sample size.

The scale does not change the coefficients, because a weighted fit does not change under a constant scale of its weights. The Kish count is the correct count for observations that are drawn alike and then reweighted. The scenario probabilities and the decay weights of the library are such weights. The count is $T$ under uniform weights, so the fit equals the unweighted fit. It is $k$ under equal weights on $k$ of the rows, so the fit equals the fit of those rows alone.

docs/adr/0203-a-weighted-regression-reads-the-kish-count-of-its-weights.md records the choice and the null test that measured it. A caller who wants another count states it with StatsBase.fweights.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} T_{e} &= \frac{\left(\sum_{t} w_{t}\right)^2}{\sum_{t} w_{t}^2}\,,\\ \tilde{w}_{t} &= w_{t} \frac{T_{e}}{\sum_{s} w_{s}} = w_{t} \frac{\sum_{s} w_{s}}{\sum_{s} w_{s}^2}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $w_{t}$: Observation weight of observation $t$.
  • $T_{e}$: Effective sample size, which is Kish's when the observation weights are stated.
  • $\tilde{w}_{t}$: Frequency weight of observation $t$. The frequency weights sum to $T_{e}$.

Arguments

  • w: The resolved observation weights, one per row of the design.

Returns

  • w::StatsBase.FrequencyWeights: w itself when it is StatsBase.FrequencyWeights, otherwise $\tilde{w}$.

Refuse a factor returns matrix with a non-finite entry, for a regression on matrices.

A factor return is a regressor of every asset, so a gap at one observation leaves that observation with no regressor for any asset.
