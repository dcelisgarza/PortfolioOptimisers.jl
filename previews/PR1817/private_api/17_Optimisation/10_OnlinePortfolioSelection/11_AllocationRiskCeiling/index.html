Refuses a risk measure that cannot be the ceiling of a ProgrammeAllocationSet.

The ceiling of a measure is its settings.ub, and a one-step projection can bound the measure by one number only. The function refuses a measure with no ub, with a Frontier or with a per-asset vector, and it refuses an empty vector of measures. A set with no measure passes.

The ceiling reads every row that the head holds, and those rows change at every step. A ReturnsTracking series is fixed at construction, so it cannot follow them, and the function refuses a measure that holds one. A WeightsTracking benchmark reads the rows themselves and passes.

Validation

  • settings.ub of a measure is not a finite non-negative number. An ArgumentError is thrown.

  • rs is empty. An IsEmptyError is thrown.

PortfolioOptimisers.resolve_block_entry — Function
resolve_block_entry(f, args::Tuple, imsk::Option{BitVector}, blocks::Tuple,
                    rd::ReturnsResult, strict::Bool;
                    rmsk::Option{BitVector} = nothing) -> Tuple

Resolves every block of a head at its entry, with one refusal branch for the readers of every block. This is the shared entry for a list of blocks.

Two heads call it. resolve_jump_optimiser passes the block of a JuMP head as the first block, and the blocks of the sets after it. projection_programme is a bare head: it holds no block of its own, and it passes the block of its set alone, on the rows of the step. The function f resolves the block of the head at a mask. It takes the mask and args..., and it returns the values of the head and a Tuple of the Results of its blocks, one Result or none. no_head_block is the f of a bare head. The function takes f and its arguments, not a closure, as distance_reader_call does, so a variable of the entry that it reads is not boxed.

A distance reader of a block, its cte or its ple, can refuse an asset that its prior keeps, see DistanceReaderRefusalError. The function then fits again without that asset, with the mask of distance_reader_refusal_mask in rmsk, from the same prior results. That mask already holds the masks of every block, so no mask warns a second time. Two readers can refuse different assets, and the fit again meets the refusal of the other one. The first refusal in the order of the blocks wins.

Algorithm

  1. On the first fit, intersect imsk with the mask of each entry of blocks, allocation_set_entry_mask. On a fit again, take rmsk.
  2. Resolve the block of the head with f(imsk, args...). Resolve each entry of blocks at the mask with resolve_allocation_set_block.
  3. If a block gives a DistanceReaderRefusalError, take the strict flag of its owner: strict for the block of the head, and projection_step_strict for the block of a set. Fit again from step 1 with the mask of distance_reader_refusal_mask, and return its value.
  4. Return the mask, the values of the head and the Results of every block.

Arguments

  • f: The resolver of the block of the head, f(imsk, args...) -> (head, cbrs::Tuple).
  • args: The arguments of f after the mask.
  • imsk: The Investable Mask of the head, or nothing when every asset is investable.
  • blocks: The fitted entries (set, X, pr) of the sets, from fit_allocation_set_block.
  • rd: The rows of the head on its full universe, read for the asset names of a refusal.
  • strict: The strict flag of the readers of the block of the head.
  • rmsk: The mask of a fit again after a refusal, or nothing on the first fit.

Validation

  • The rules of allocation_set_entry_mask and distance_reader_refusal_mask.

Returns

  • (imsk, head, cbrs)::Tuple: The Investable Mask of the entry, the values that f gave for the head, and the Tuple of the block Results, the block of the head first.

PortfolioOptimisers.set_allocation_risk_ceiling! — Function
set_allocation_risk_ceiling!(model::JuMP.Model, r::Nothing, set::ProgrammeAllocationSet, pr, pl; prefix::Symbol = Symbol(""))
set_allocation_risk_ceiling!(model::JuMP.Model, r::RiskMeasure, set::ProgrammeAllocationSet, pr, pl; prefix::Symbol = Symbol(""))

set_allocation_risk_ceiling!(model::JuMP.Model, rs::VecRM, set::ProgrammeAllocationSet, pr::AbstractPriorResult, pl; prefix::Symbol = Symbol(""))

Adds a programme set's risk ceilings to the model, one row block for each measure, at the measure's index.

Each ceiling goes through the measure's own JuMP builder, set_risk_constraints!, with the set as the RiskConstraintOwner. The builder bounds each measure at its settings.ub through set_risk_upper_bound!, and its own # JuMP formulation names the rows. So the set writes the rows that a JuMP optimiser writes for the same bound. A Variance takes the semidefinite formulation under the conditions that select it in a head, sdp_variance_flag!: risk-contribution rows, an earlier variance with such rows on the same weights, or a semidefinite phylogeny in pl. Otherwise it takes the second-order cone. The variance marks variance_flag, so a semidefinite phylogeny on the same weights adds no p · tr(W) penalty, as in a head.

The source of the covariance matrix does not change the formulation. With a prior result, each measure is resolved against it first. Without one, every ceiling is a Variance or a StandardDeviation that holds its matrix, on a set that reads no rows, and the builder reads that matrix with pr = nothing.

Under a prefix, the builders put their entries in that namespace. The block builder set_block_constraints! registers the head's w under the prefix before any builder runs. This keeps the ceiling's entries apart from the entries of the head's own measures. The lifted matrix $\mathbf{W}$ and the marks belong to the weights, weights_prefix, so in a leader's model the ceiling, the set's phylogeny and the leader's measures read one $\mathbf{W}$.

Algorithm

For one measure:

  1. Wrap r in a vector and call the vector method.

For a vector and a prior result:

  1. Resolve each measure against pr with factory and set.slv. A moment that the measure holds, such as its own covariance, stays. A cache that the head filled from another prior is not read.
  2. Clear the measure's rke with no_risk_expr_risk_measure, so its expression joins no objective. The measure's scale weights its expression only in the objective, so a ceiling ignores it.
  3. Call set_risk_constraints! on the vector of ceilings, with the set as owner, pr, pl and prefix.

For a vector and no prior result:

  1. For each measure at index i, clear its rke, as above.
  2. Call the index-aware set_risk_constraints! at i, with the set as owner, pr = nothing, pl and prefix.

Arguments

  • model::JuMP.Model: The JuMP optimisation model.
  • r: The set's risk measure, nothing, or a vector of measures.
  • set: The resolved programme Allocation Set.
  • pr: The set's prior result, or nothing when the set reads no rows.
  • pl: The set's resolved phylogeny constraints, or nothing.
  • prefix: The Model State namespace of the entries, Symbol("") on the bare projection model.

Returns

  • nothing.

PortfolioOptimisers.clip_at_zero — Function
clip_at_zero(w::AbstractVector, lb = zero(eltype(w)))

Sets every entry of a projection programme's answer at or below the tolerance of its element type to its floor lb, which is zero unless the set states a positive floor.

A geometry whose domain is the non-negative orthant needs this. The solver meets a zero lower bound only to within its tolerance, so a leg that the programme closes comes back slightly above or slightly below zero. The next step forms its raw step multiplicatively from the allocation it holds. It would take log w of a negative number, and an exact zero of the raw step keeps the weight at zero. So the clip reads every entry within the tolerance as zero, whatever the sign of the rounding, and a weight that the set forces to zero is zero after the clip. The tolerance is weights_bound_tolerance: $\sqrt{\epsilon}$ for a floating-point type, which is above the default feasibility tolerance of Clarabel, and zero for a Rational. The clip does not renormalise, so the sum moves by the clipped mass, which is at most $N \tau$. An entry whose floor is positive goes to that floor and not to zero, so the clip keeps a floor at or below $\tau$ that the set states.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} w'_i &= \begin{cases} l_i & w_i \leq \tau\,, \\ w_i & w_i > \tau\,. \end{cases} \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $w_i$: Entry $i$ of the programme's answer.
  • $w'_i$: Entry $i$ of the clipped answer.
  • $l_i$: Entry $i$ of the floor lb, non-negative.
  • $\tau$: The tolerance that weights_bound_tolerance gives for the element type of $\boldsymbol{w}$, which is $\sqrt{\epsilon}$ for a floating-point type and zero for an exact type such as Rational.
  • $N$: Number of assets.

Arguments

  • w: The programme's answer.

Refuses a return term whose floor a ProgrammeAllocationSet cannot hold.

The floor of a term is its settings.lb. A Frontier or a vector of floors asks for one allocation at each floor, and a one-step projection returns one allocation. The model would hold the floors in :ret_frontier, and no solve would read them. So the function refuses both forms, as assert_risk_ceiling refuses them for a ceiling. A floor that is nothing or a number passes.

Arguments

  • ret: The return floor of the set, nothing, or a vector of return terms.

Validation

  • settings.lb of a term is nothing or a number. An ArgumentError is thrown otherwise.

Returns

  • nothing.
