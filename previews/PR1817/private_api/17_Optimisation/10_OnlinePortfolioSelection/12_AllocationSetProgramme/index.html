PortfolioOptimisers.set_projection_objective! — Function
set_projection_objective!(model::JuMP.Model, proj::EuclideanProjection, q::AbstractVector)
set_projection_objective!(model::JuMP.Model, proj::EntropicProjection, q::AbstractVector)

set_projection_objective!(model::JuMP.Model, proj::TsallisProjection, q::AbstractVector)
set_projection_objective!(model::JuMP.Model, proj::LogBarrierProjection, q::AbstractVector)

Sets the objective of the projection programme to the geometry's divergence from the raw step q, plus the Objective Penalty.

Each arm writes its divergence $f$ as a conic model and hands it to set_divergence_objective!, which adds the penalty and sets the objective.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} f_{\mathrm{E}}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \lVert \boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{q} \rVert_2\,, \\ f_{\mathrm{E}}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \frac{1}{2} \lVert \boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{q} \rVert_2^2\,, \\ f_{\mathrm{G}}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \lVert \mathbf{G} (\boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{q}) \rVert_2\,, \\ f_{\mathrm{G}}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \frac{1}{2} \lVert \mathbf{G} (\boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{q}) \rVert_2^2\,, \\ f_{\mathrm{KL}}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \sum_{i \in P} w_i \log \frac{w_i}{q_i}\,, \\ f_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \frac{1}{1 - \alpha} \sum_{i \in P} \left( q_i^{\alpha - 1} w_i - \frac{w_i^{\alpha}}{\alpha} \right)\,, \\ f_{\mathrm{IS}}(\boldsymbol{w}) &= \sum_{i \in P} \left( \frac{w_i}{q_i} - \log w_i \right)\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{w}$: Portfolio weights vector $N \times 1$.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}$: Raw step, the vector the rule's unconstrained step gives before the projection onto the Allocation Set.
  • $\pi$: Objective Penalty, the sum of the set's penalties and of the other terms added to the objective before it.
  • $f_{\mathrm{E}}$, $f_{\mathrm{E}}^{\pi}$: Divergence of EuclideanProjection, without and with an Objective Penalty.
  • $f_{\mathrm{G}}$, $f_{\mathrm{G}}^{\pi}$: Divergence of GramProjection and of DiagonalProjection, without and with an Objective Penalty.
  • $\mathbf{G}$: Upper Cholesky factor of the geometry's Gram matrix $\mathbf{A}$, so $\mathbf{G}^\intercal \mathbf{G} = \mathbf{A}$. Under DiagonalProjection it is $\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\boldsymbol{h}})$ and $\mathbf{A} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{h})$.
  • $f_{\mathrm{KL}}$: Relative entropy, the divergence of EntropicProjection.
  • $f_{\alpha}$: Tsallis divergence, the divergence of TsallisProjection.
  • $\alpha$: Power of the Tsallis potential, in $(0, 1)$.
  • $f_{\mathrm{IS}}$: Itakura–Saito divergence, the divergence of LogBarrierProjection.
  • $P = \{i : q_i > 0\}$: Positive entries of the raw step. Under the entropic, Tsallis and log-barrier geometries every $w_i$ with $i \notin P$ is zero.
  • $\Psi$: Potential of the Projection Geometry.
  • $D_\Psi$: Bregman divergence of $\Psi$.

Each $f$ differs from the Bregman divergence $D_\Psi(\boldsymbol{w}, \boldsymbol{q})$ of its geometry's potential by a term that is constant in $\boldsymbol{w}$ on the budget $\sum_i w_i = 1$, so each has the minimiser of $D_\Psi$ and a penalty added to it weighs against $D_\Psi$ itself. $f_{\mathrm{E}}^{\pi}$ and $f_{\mathrm{G}}^{\pi}$ are these Bregman divergences, of $\tfrac{1}{2} \lVert \boldsymbol{w} \rVert_2^2$ and $\tfrac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{w}^\intercal \mathbf{A} \boldsymbol{w}$. The norm $f_{\mathrm{E}}$ has the same minimiser as $f_{\mathrm{E}}^{\pi}$ when no penalty is present, and $f_{\mathrm{G}}$ has the same minimiser as $f_{\mathrm{G}}^{\pi}$. A penalty $\pi$ added to the norm does not move the minimiser off a feasible $\boldsymbol{q}$ while the dual norm of $\nabla \pi(\boldsymbol{q})$ is at most one, because the subdifferential of a norm at zero is its dual unit ball. Added to the squared norm, it gives the proximal step.

JuMP formulation

Variables

  • w: read from the model.
  • t_proj: created. It is a scalar under the Euclidean, Gram, diagonal and entropic arms, and a vector with one entry for each index in $P$ under the Tsallis and log-barrier arms.

Constraints

  • Euclidean, Gram and diagonal arms: the row of set_distance_cone! on $\boldsymbol{x} = s_c (\boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{q})$ or $\boldsymbol{x} = s_c \mathbf{G} (\boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{q})$.
  • Entropic arm, proj_zero: $s_c w_i = 0$ for each $i \notin P$, when such an $i$ exists.
  • Entropic arm, proj_rec: $s_c t \geq \sum_{i \in P} s_c w_i \log (w_i / q_i)$, the relative entropy cone over $(s_c t, s_c \boldsymbol{q}_P, s_c \boldsymbol{w}_P)$.
  • Tsallis arm, under no name: $t_j \leq w_j^{\alpha}$ for each $j \in P$, the power cone of power $\alpha$ over $(s_c w_j, s_c, s_c t_j)$.
  • Log-barrier arm, under no name: $t_j \leq \log w_j$ for each $j \in P$, the exponential cone over $(s_c t_j, s_c, s_c w_j)$.
  • Tsallis and log-barrier arms: the rows of barrier_objective_entries, which fix $w_i = 0$ for each $i \notin P$.

The divergence each arm hands to set_divergence_objective! is $t$ under the Euclidean, Gram, diagonal and entropic arms, $\frac{1}{1 - \alpha} \left( \sum_{j \in P} q_j^{\alpha - 1} w_j - \frac{1}{\alpha} \sum_{j \in P} t_j \right)$ under the Tsallis arm, and $\sum_{j \in P} w_j / q_j - \sum_{j \in P} t_j$ under the log-barrier arm.

Where:

  • $s_c$: Constraint scale. It multiplies both sides of a row, so a positive value leaves the feasible set unchanged.
  • $t$, $t_j$: The entries of t_proj.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}_P$, $\boldsymbol{w}_P$: The entries of $\boldsymbol{q}$ and $\boldsymbol{w}$ at the indices in $P$.

Arguments

  • model::JuMP.Model: The JuMP optimisation model.
  • proj: The Projection Geometry.
  • q: The raw step.

Validation

  • Under EntropicProjection, TsallisProjection and LogBarrierProjection: all(>= 0, q) and sum(q) > 0. A DomainError is thrown otherwise.
  • Under GramProjection: proj.A is not nothing. An ArgumentError is thrown otherwise: the rule binds the matrix.
  • Under GramProjection: proj.A is positive definite. A LinearAlgebra.PosDefException is thrown otherwise. A singular Gram matrix states a seminorm, in which the projection is not unique. NewtonStep binds $\mathbf{I} + \sum_{s} \boldsymbol{g}_s \boldsymbol{g}_s^\intercal$, which is positive definite.

PortfolioOptimisers.projection_programme — Function
projection_programme(
    proj::AbstractProjectionGeometry,
    set::AbstractAllocationSet,
    q::AbstractVector,
    w::AbstractVector
) -> Any

Projects the raw step q onto the Allocation Set in the geometry's divergence, as a JuMP programme.

A solved programme returns its weights. A programme that does not solve, or whose constraints cannot be built on the step's rows (allocation_set_ready), is a Held Step. Then record_held_step! writes the record to the current ProjectionStep, and the function returns a copy of w, the book the fund already holds.

A set that reads the rows fits its prior on the rows of the step, as a batch head does. The programme then runs on the prior's Investable Mask, also as a batch head does. It solves over the assets the prior prices, and gives a zero weight to every other asset. Under a time-varying panel, an asset without a listing for part of the window is outside the universe of a plain prior until the window no longer holds that part. So the programme gives it a zero, which is the one zero in the recursion's allocation that the rule's step did not make. A batch head gives the same zero. The asset comes back at the first step at which the prior prices it, and the rule's geometry decides whether the raw step then gives it weight, as for a zero in the Start Allocation. A set that reads no rows fits no prior and solves over the full universe.

The programme is a bare head of the shared entry resolve_block_entry: it holds no block of its own, and it passes the block of its set. So the mask of the step follows the rules of the entry of a JuMP head. An asset that a FeatureDistance of a reader of the set cannot read, an asset with a gap in the rows that a plain graph reader of the set reads, and an asset that a distance reader of the set refuses each take a zero weight at the step, with a warning. Under the strict flag of the step, the programme refuses them.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \boldsymbol{w}^{+} &= \underset{\boldsymbol{w} \in \mathcal{W}}{\arg\min} \; D_\Psi(\boldsymbol{w}, \boldsymbol{q}) + \pi(\boldsymbol{w})\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{w}^{+}$: Projected allocation, the answer of the projection onto the Allocation Set.
  • $\mathcal{W}$: Allocation Set. Under an Investable Mask it also holds $w_i = 0$ for every asset $i$ outside the mask.
  • $D_\Psi$: Bregman divergence of $\Psi$.
  • $\Psi$: Potential of the Projection Geometry.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}$: Raw step, the vector the rule's unconstrained step gives before the projection onto the Allocation Set.

Resolves the Allocation Set of a projection programme for one step, through the shared entry of a bare head.

The projection programme holds no block of its own, so it is a bare head of resolve_block_entry, with no_head_block as the resolver of its block. It passes the block of its set as the one block. The set is the owner of the prior of the step, so the mask of that prior is the mask of the bare head, and the set prices every asset that the head keeps. The entry then composes the mask with the readers of the set, resolves the block at the mask, and fits again without an asset that a distance reader of the set refuses, as for the blocks of a JuMP head. A bounded set holds no block and fits no prior, so it resolves to itself. A programme set with no rows, X === nothing outside an Online Update, fits no prior, so it has no mask and runs no reader, and stage 2 of its block resolves alone. allocation_set_prior refuses such a set when a slot reads the rows.

Algorithm

  1. On a bounded set, return no mask, the set and no block Result. Stop.
  2. Give the set the Price-Adjusted Allocation w with factory(set, w), so a turnover ceiling, a tracking error, a risk ceiling, a custom constraint and a custom objective term that read the previous weights read the book of the step.
  3. With no rows, resolve the block with resolve_constraint_block on no prior, check the bounds with assert_feasible_bounds, and return no mask, the set and the block Result. Stop.
  4. Fit the prior of the set on X with fit_allocation_set_block.
  5. Resolve the block through resolve_block_entry, from the mask of the prior, prior_investable_mask, under the strict flag of the step, projection_step_strict.
  6. Check the bounds of the step against the budget of one with assert_feasible_bounds. A bound that reads the rows resolves only at stage 2 (bounds_read_rows).
  7. View the set at the mask with programme_investable_reduction, for the builders. Return the mask, the viewed set and the block Result.

Arguments

  • set: The set, resolved once per fold.
  • w: The Price-Adjusted Allocation the step trades from.
  • X: The rows the head holds through the period, a ReturnsResult, or nothing.

Validation

  • The rules of allocation_set_prior, resolve_block_entry and assert_feasible_bounds.
  • Under the strict flag of the step, no reader of the set drops an asset. Raises an IsNonFiniteError.

Returns

  • (imsk, set', cbr)::Tuple: The Investable Mask of the step, the set viewed at it, and the ResolvedConstraintBlock of the set, or nothing for a bounded set.

Reduces the set, the rows that the head holds and the set's prior to the prior's Investable Mask.

The projection programme then runs on the assets the prior can price, as a batch head does with investable_reduction. The shared entry reduces the block of a set to the mask of every block in this way, resolve_allocation_set_block, and resolve_projection_entry views the set for the builders of the projection. The projection views the raw step at the same mask with investable_geometry_view.

Dispatch selects one of three methods. A nothing mask means that every asset is investable, which is true at every step on a static panel and at every step of a set that reads no rows. That method returns its arguments unchanged. The BitVector method takes the views at findall(imsk). A set that reads no rows has no prior, and the method views the set and the rows alone. This pair occurs at the entry of a JuMP head, whose own prior gives the mask. The third method refuses a mask with no rows, because a mask is read on the rows. The Price-Adjusted Allocation is cut to the mask and is not renormalised, as the batch reduction cuts a turnover reference. So an asset the prior cannot price leaves the reference, and the programme allocates the budget of one over the investable assets. The function writes no warning for such an asset, because the Investable Mask of the Recursion Result already shows it at every step.

investable_geometry_view(imsk::BitVector, proj::AbstractProjectionGeometry, q::AbstractVector)
investable_geometry_view(imsk::BitVector, proj::DiagonalProjection, q::AbstractVector)
investable_geometry_view(imsk::BitVector, proj::GramProjection{<:Any, <:AbstractMatrix}, q::AbstractVector)

Reduces the Projection Geometry and the raw step q to the Investable Mask of a projection programme.

projection_programme solves over the assets in the mask, and gives a zero weight to every other asset. A nothing mask returns both arguments unchanged. A geometry whose divergence is a sum of one term for each asset needs only the view of q, because the term of an asset outside the mask is a constant at its zero weight. DiagonalProjection is such a sum, and its diagonal is viewed with q. The norm of a GramProjection couples the assets, so the zero weights move the point that the reduced programme projects.

Mathematical definition

Let $P$ be the assets in the mask and $Z$ the others, with $\boldsymbol{w}_Z = \boldsymbol{0}$. Then

\[\begin{align} (\boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{q})^\intercal \mathbf{A} (\boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{q}) &= (\boldsymbol{w}_P - \tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}_P)^\intercal \mathbf{A}_{PP} (\boldsymbol{w}_P - \tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}_P) + c\,, \\ \tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}_P &= \boldsymbol{q}_P + \mathbf{A}_{PP}^{-1} \mathbf{A}_{PZ} \boldsymbol{q}_Z\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{w}$: Portfolio weights vector $N \times 1$.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}$: Raw step, the vector the rule's unconstrained step gives before the projection onto the Allocation Set.
  • $\mathbf{A}$: Gram matrix of the geometry, positive definite.
  • $\mathbf{A}_{PP}$, $\mathbf{A}_{PZ}$: Blocks of $\mathbf{A}$ at the rows $P$ and the columns $P$ and $Z$. $\mathbf{A}_{PP}$ is positive definite, because $\mathbf{A}$ is.
  • $\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}_P$: Raw step of the reduced programme.
  • $c$: Term that does not depend on $\boldsymbol{w}_P$.

So the reduced Gram geometry holds $\mathbf{A}_{PP}$ and projects $\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}_P$. The Gram method reads a bound matrix. NewtonStep, the one rule that takes a GramProjection, binds the matrix before the view through gram_geometry.

Returns

  • (proj, q)::Tuple: The geometry and the raw step on the assets in the mask.

PortfolioOptimisers.solve_projection_programme! — Function
solve_projection_programme!(model::JuMP.Model, proj::AbstractProjectionGeometry, q::AbstractVector, slv::Slv_VecSlv)
solve_projection_programme!(model::JuMP.Model, proj::DiagonalProjection, q::AbstractVector, slv::Slv_VecSlv)

Solves a projection programme, and picks one minimiser when the divergence of the geometry has many.

projection_programme calls it after it writes the objective. Every geometry except DiagonalProjection has one minimiser, and the function solves the model once with optimise_JuMP_model!. A diagonal with a zero entry is a seminorm, so its minimisers share their weights at the positive entries of the diagonal and differ at the zero entries. After a first solve that succeeds, the diagonal arm then solves a second stage on the same model. Its answer is the limit of the projection in $\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{h} + \delta \boldsymbol{1})$ as $\delta \to 0^{+}$: the minimiser nearest $\boldsymbol{q}$ in the Euclidean norm.

When the second stage fails, the arm returns the weights of the first stage, and the result is a success that holds the trials of the second stage. Those weights are a minimiser of the projection, so the regret bound of AdaptiveSubgradient holds on them. The second stage can fail when its face is one point: under a strictly convex Objective Penalty, or when the budget pins the free weights at their bounds. That point is then the limit. On a face of more than one point, the weights of the first stage are a minimiser that need not be the limit.

JuMP formulation

The second stage of the diagonal arm, when $Z$ is not empty.

Variables

  • w: read from the model.
  • t_sel: created.

Constraints

  • proj_face: $s_o \, (d + \pi) \leq v$, when the model holds an Objective Penalty. It keeps the objective of the first stage at its minimum, so the penalty stays at its minimum too. The point of the first stage meets it, so the second stage is feasible to the tolerance of the solver. A slack would let the free weights move along the curve of the penalty by the square root of the slack, so the bound has none.
  • proj_fix: $s_c (w_i - w_i^{(1)}) = 0$ for each $i \in P$.
  • proj_sel: $(s_c t, s_c (\boldsymbol{w}_Z - \boldsymbol{q}_Z)) \in \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{SOC}}$.

Objective

  • Min: $s_o t$.

Where:

  • $P = \{i : h_i > 0\}$, $Z = \{i : h_i = 0\}$: Assets at the positive and at the zero entries of the diagonal.
  • $w_i^{(1)}$: Weight of asset $i$ at the first stage, which every minimiser shares.
  • $v$: Objective value of the first stage.
  • $d + \pi$: The expression obj_expr of the first stage, the divergence and the Objective Penalty.
  • $t$: The variable t_sel.
  • $s_c$: Constraint scale. It multiplies both sides of a row, so a positive value leaves the feasible set unchanged.
  • $s_o$: Objective scale. It multiplies the objective, so a positive value leaves the argument of the optimum unchanged.
  • $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{SOC}}$: Second-order cone.

Arguments

  • model::JuMP.Model: The JuMP optimisation model.
  • proj: The geometry, reduced to the Investable Mask.
  • q: The raw step, reduced to the Investable Mask.
  • slv: The solver of the programme.

Returns

  • (res, w)::Tuple: res::JuMPResult is the result of the last solve, and a first solve that fails is the last one. w::Option{<:AbstractVector} is the solved allocation, or nothing when no solve gives one.
