PortfolioOptimisers.resolve_constraint_block_names — Function
resolve_constraint_block_names(
    cbe::ConstraintBlock,
    strict::Bool,
    datatype::DataType
) -> ConstraintBlock{_A, _B, _C, _D, _E, _F, _G, _H, _I, _J, _K, _L, _M, _N, _O, _P, _Q, _R, _S, _T, _U, _V, _W, _X, _Y, _Z, _Z1, _Z2, _Z3, _Z4, _Z5, Bool} where {_A, _B, _C, _D, _E, _F, _G, _H, _I, _J, _K, _L, _M, _N, _O, _P, _Q, _R, _S, _T, _U, _V, _W, _X, _Y, _Z, _Z1, _Z2, _Z3, _Z4, _Z5}

Resolves the slots of a ConstraintBlock that hold a name or an estimator over sets. This is stage 1 of the resolution.

Stage 1 is configuration. It gives the block with values in its slots, and it holds no Result. It runs before the reduction to the Investable Mask, over the universe of the caller, where every name of sets is an asset of the caller. The reduction then views each value at the mask, see port_opt_view(cbe::ConstraintBlock, i, args...). A JuMP head runs it once for each optimise call, and an online head runs it once for each fold, in resolve_allocation_set.

A slot is a stage 1 slot when its value follows a view at the mask, so the two orders of the names and the reduction give one value. The other slots resolve at stage 2, in resolve_constraint_block, after the reduction:

  • wb and lcse, because their resolvers read the rows rd.
  • gcarde, because it resolves to a LinearConstraint over the assets, and a view does not narrow its matrix. At stage 2 it resolves over the sets that carry the Non-Investable Axis.
  • cte, ple and the slots that can hold a Calibration Rule, because they read the prior.

Algorithm

  1. Resolve the thresholds lt and st with threshold_constraints.
  2. Resolve the sub-grouped cardinality constraint sgcarde with linear_constraints, with integer values. Its columns are the sub-groups and not the assets, so a view does not change it.
  3. Resolve the sub-group matrices smtx and sgmtx with asset_sets_matrix, and the sub-group thresholds slt, sst, sglt and sgst with threshold_constraints. When the two slots of a pair hold one object, resolve it once with shared_pair_resolution.
  4. Resolve the turnover constraints tn with turnover_constraints.
  5. Return cbe rebuilt with the resolved slots, with rebuild_estimator.

Arguments

  • cbe: The block.
  • strict: Whether to throw an error if sets does not contain the desired value in sets.dict[key].
  • datatype: The element type of the resolved values.

Returns

  • cbe'::ConstraintBlock: The block with its stage 1 slots resolved.

PortfolioOptimisers.resolve_constraint_block — Function
resolve_constraint_block(cbe::ConstraintBlock, pr::AbstractPriorResult, rd::ReturnsResult;
                         slv, strict::Bool = false, kwargs...) -> ResolvedConstraintBlock
resolve_constraint_block(cbe::ConstraintBlock, pr::Nothing, rd::Option{<:ReturnsResult};
                         strict::Bool = false, kwargs...) -> ResolvedConstraintBlock
resolve_constraint_block(cbe::ConstraintBlock, pr::AbstractPriorResult, rd::Nothing;
                         kwargs...)

Resolves a ConstraintBlock against its own prior and rows, to its ResolvedConstraintBlock. This is stage 2 of the resolution.

Both owners of a block call it. resolve_jump_optimiser calls it once for each optimise call, and resolve_allocation_set_step calls it at each step of an online head. The block must hold the result of stage 1, resolve_constraint_block_names, viewed at the Investable Mask of pr. So pr, rd and the block are on the investable assets. A slot that still holds an estimator of stage 1 raises a TypeError in the constructor of the Result.

One rule orders the names and the reduction for every block. Stage 1 resolves before the reduction, and a view at the mask follows its values. This function resolves after the reduction, over the sets of the block, which carry the Non-Investable Axis. So a name that left the investable universe drops its row and is not refused, and a precomputed constraint keeps its width. assert_investable_constraint_width then refuses a precomputed lcse or gcarde whose width is not the number of assets of the block.

The method for pr = nothing is the method of a programme set that reads no rows, see rows_needed. Its prior-reading slots hold values that read no prior, so it resolves the slots that read names alone, and keeps every other slot. Its Result holds no prior and no rows.

Algorithm

The method on a prior result runs these steps.

  1. Resolve the weight bounds wb with weight_bounds_constraints, with the rows rd and the N assets of pr.X.
  2. Resolve lcse with linear_constraints, with the factor regression pr.rr and the rows, and gcarde with integer values. Check the width of both results with assert_investable_constraint_width.
  3. Fit the centrality rows ctr with centrality_constraints and the phylogeny result plr with phylogeny_constraints. Each reads the prior, the rows and the implied volatilities rd.iv and rd.ivpa. A reader that cannot estimate an asset raises a DistanceReaderRefusalError, which the caller catches.
  4. Resolve each Calibration Rule against the prior and the solver slv. The norm ceilings l2c and linfc state their norm order in a CalibrationContext, and lpc goes through norm_ceiling_factory. The penalties l1 and linf go through resolve_calibration_slot, and l2 and lp through their own factory.
  5. Check the four slots that hold a number, l2c, linfc, l1 and linf, with assert_nonempty_gt0_finite_val. A rule states no number at construction, so this is the first point that knows the number.
  6. Return the Result, with the stage 1 slots and the settings of cbe, pr and rd.

Arguments

  • cbe: The block, after stage 1 and the view at the mask.
  • pr: The prior result of the block, or nothing when the block reads no rows.
  • rd: The rows of the block. The method for pr = nothing reads only their element type and their width, and it takes nothing outside an Online Update.
  • slv: The solver of the owner, which a Calibration Rule resolves against.
  • strict: Whether to throw an error if sets does not contain the desired value in sets.dict[key].
  • kwargs...: Passed to centrality_constraints and phylogeny_constraints.

Validation

  • The rules of assert_investable_constraint_width for lcsr and gcardr. The method for pr = nothing checks them when it has rows.
  • l2c, linfc, l1 and linf are nothing or finite and > 0 after the resolution. A DomainError or an IsNonFiniteError is thrown otherwise.
  • A prior result with no rows. An IsNothingError is thrown. The prior of a block is fitted on its rows, so this pair does not occur in the library.

Returns

  • cbr::ResolvedConstraintBlock: The Result of the block.

PortfolioOptimisers.set_block_constraints! — Function
set_block_constraints!(model::JuMP.Model, cbr::ResolvedConstraintBlock, owner, bgt, fees;

                       prefix::Symbol = Symbol(""), kwargs...) -> Nothing
set_block_post_risk_constraints!(model::JuMP.Model, cbr::ResolvedConstraintBlock, owner,
                                 attrs; prefix::Symbol = Symbol("")) -> Nothing

Add a ResolvedConstraintBlock to a model, in its own namespace and in two phases. This is the block builder.

Every programme that holds a block adds it through these two functions: a JuMP head in assemble_jump_model! for its own block and for each later block, such as the set in the model of a leader, and the projection of a ProgrammeAllocationSet in set_allocation_set_constraints!. So the order of the Model Assembly is written once. The owner of the model runs its own steps between the two phases: the fees, the risk step, the return terms and mark_risk_minimised!.

Phase 1, set_block_constraints!, adds the rows that come before the risk step of the owner. Phase 2, set_block_post_risk_constraints!, adds the rows that read the marks of the objective of the owner, risk_minimised and variance_flag: the semidefinite phylogeny reads them. The integer phylogeny and the custom constraints come after them too, as they did in each owner.

The first block of a model takes the empty prefix and the names of today. Each block after the first takes a Model State namespace. In the model of a JuMP head, block i takes blk_i_, see later_constraint_blocks. Phase 1 registers the weights of the model under it, with the owner w_owner = Symbol(""), so weights_prefix keeps the lifted matrix and the marks with the weights. Every builder then registers its entries under the prefix. A kind on two blocks gives two sets of rows and no name collision. The MIP bits, the long and short parts of the weights and the pin of xbgt belong to the weights, so the blocks share them. The penalties of each block add to the one Objective Penalty.

The block reads its own prior cbr.pr and its own phylogeny cbr.plr. The builder takes the values of the owner that the block cannot hold: the net budget bgt, for the long-short and gross budgets, the fees, for the MIP kinds and the tracking errors, the tracking errors tr that the owner bound to its rows, and the factor loadings b1 of a factor head. The custom constraints get the owner and the bundle attrs that the owner gives them. A block of a set passes the set as the owner, nothing as the net budget and the fees, and nothing as attrs.

Algorithm

Phase 1:

  1. When prefix is not empty, register the weights of the model under it, with the owner Symbol(""). Add the weight bounds, the net budget bgt, and the short and gross budgets of the block. set_block_weight_constraints! does both.
  2. Add the linear rows lcsr, then the centrality rows ctr, with set_linear_weight_constraints!.
  3. Add the MIP kinds with set_mip_constraints!, then the sub-group MIP kinds with set_smip_constraints!.
  4. Add the turnover ceilings with set_turnover_constraints!.
  5. Add the tracking errors tr with set_tracking_error_constraints!, on the prior of the block.
  6. Add the norm ceilings l2c, lpc and linfc, then the penalties l1, l2, lp and linf.

Phase 2:

  1. Add the integer phylogeny rows with set_iplg_constraints!, then the semidefinite phylogeny rows with set_sdp_phylogeny_constraints!.
  2. Add the custom constraints ccnt with add_custom_constraint!.

Arguments

  • model::JuMP.Model: The JuMP optimisation model.
  • cbr: The Result of the block.
  • owner: The owner of the block: a JuMP head, or a ProgrammeAllocationSet. The tracking errors and the custom constraints dispatch on it.
  • bgt: The net budget of the owner of the block, or nothing.
  • fees: The fees of the owner of the block, or nothing.
  • tr: The tracking errors of the owner, bound to its rows, or nothing.
  • b1::Option{<:MatNum} = nothing: Basis of the decision vector of FactorRiskContribution, which maps it to the asset weights: the pseudoinverse of the transposed loadings, followed by the off-factor basis when flag = true. nothing for all other optimisers.
  • attrs: The bundle that the custom constraints read, or nothing.
  • prefix::Symbol = Symbol(""): Model State namespace of the call. The builder reads the weights under it, and every entry that it registers takes it, so two calls under two prefixes add two sets of rows to one model. The empty default gives the bare names.
  • kwargs...: Passed to set_tracking_error_constraints!. A JuMP head passes its rows as rd.

Returns

  • nothing.

set_block_objective_terms!(model::JuMP.Model, cbr::Nothing, obj, owner) -> Nothing
set_block_objective_terms!(model::JuMP.Model, cbr::ResolvedConstraintBlock, obj, owner) -> Nothing

Add the custom objective terms cobj of a ResolvedConstraintBlock to the Objective Penalty of a model. This is the step of the block builder for the objective.

A term of a block gets the objective obj of the model, the owner of its block and the Result of its block, through add_custom_objective_term!. The term adds to the Objective Penalty, and the objective builder of the owner adds the penalty to its objective. So the owner calls this function after the two phases of the block, set_block_constraints! and set_block_post_risk_constraints!, and before it sets the objective. The projection programme of a ProgrammeAllocationSet calls it with its Projection Geometry as obj. A nothing block, the block of a BoundedAllocationSet, adds no term.

Arguments

  • model::JuMP.Model: The JuMP optimisation model.
  • cbr: The Result of the block, or nothing when the owner holds no block.
  • obj: The objective of the model, such as a Projection Geometry.
  • owner: The owner of the block.

Returns

  • nothing.
