abstract type AbstractSequentialTailViewConstraint <: AbstractEntropyPoolingTailView

Abstract supertype for the tail view constraints of the sequential convex formulations.

A sequential tail view constraint holds a view that reads as a lower bound, and it splits the assets of the view into two sides. The risk measures of the dual side are concave in the posterior probabilities, and each takes its exact dual block. The risk measures of the primal side have a negative coefficient, and each takes a linear upper bound read at a fixed posterior. The three subtypes differ only in the risk measure. add_ep_tail_view! and ep_refine_tail_view each have one method for the whole family. Each subtype has a method of ep_tail_dual_block! and of ep_tail_surrogate_row for its risk measure.

PortfolioOptimisers.ep_tail_dual_block! — Function
ep_tail_dual_block!(model::JuMP.Model, pw, tv::AbstractEntropyPoolingTailView, x::VecNum,
                    sc1::Number)

Add the dual block of one asset's risk measure to an entropy pooling JuMP model, and return the expression that attains the measure.

The dual representation of each measure is the maximum of $\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\intercal} \boldsymbol{x}$ over a set of weights that depends on the posterior probabilities. ep_tail_dual_block! adds that set to the model and returns the linear expression. The caller bounds the expression alone, or in a coefficient-weighted sum over several assets. The tail view constraint tv names the measure and holds its level. The fixed tail view constraint of a measure and its sequential tail view constraint use the same method.

The conditional value-at-risk block is Equation 6 of [1]. The entropic block is Equation 1 of [2], and the relativistic block is Equations 4 and 7 of [2]. Cajas [2] prints the bound of its Equation 7 as 0 <= nu_j <= 0. The library reads it as the bound of Equation 4, 0 <= nu_j <= 1.

JuMP formulation

Variables

  • pw: $\boldsymbol{p}$: $T \times 1$ posterior probabilities of the observations, summing to one. They are the unknown of the entropy pooling problem. The caller gives it, and every entry below reads it.
  • nu: $\boldsymbol{\nu}$: $T \times 1$ vector of weights that attains the risk measure, the variable of its dual representation. The conditional value-at-risk method gives it a lower bound of zero, and the two other methods bound it to $[0, 1]$.
  • tau, varsigma: $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ and $\boldsymbol{\varsigma}$, two $T \times 1$ vectors with a lower bound of zero. The relativistic value-at-risk method makes them. The cones and the budget already imply both bounds. With the bounds stated, the solver reports OPTIMAL where it otherwise reports SLOW_PROGRESS.

Expressions

  • The method returns $\sum_{j=1}^{T} \nu_{j} x_{j}$, which has no name.

Constraints

No row has a name. The conditional value-at-risk method, for LinearConditionalValueatRiskViewConstraint and SequentialConditionalValueatRiskViewConstraint, registers two:

  • $s_{c1} \left(\nu_{j} - \dfrac{p_{j}}{\alpha}\right) \leq 0$, $\forall\, j = 1,\ldots,T$.
  • $s_{c1} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{T} \nu_{j} - 1\right) = 0$.

The entropic value-at-risk method, for ConicEntropicValueatRiskViewConstraint and SequentialEntropicValueatRiskViewConstraint, registers two:

  • $s_{c1} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{T} \nu_{j} - 1\right) = 0$.
  • $\left(s_{c1} \ln\left(\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\right),\, s_{c1} \boldsymbol{p},\, s_{c1} \boldsymbol{\nu}\right) \in \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{re}}(2T+1)$.

The relativistic value-at-risk method, for ConicRelativisticValueatRiskViewConstraint and SequentialRelativisticValueatRiskViewConstraint, registers four:

  • $s_{c1} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{T} \nu_{j} - 1\right) = 0$.
  • $s_{c1} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{T} \dfrac{\tau_{j} - \varsigma_{j}}{2\kappa} - \ln_{\kappa}\left(\dfrac{1}{\alpha T}\right)\right) \leq 0$.
  • $\left(s_{c1} \tau_{j},\, s_{c1} T p_{j},\, s_{c1} \nu_{j}\right) \in \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{pow}}\left(\dfrac{1}{1+\kappa}\right)$, $\forall\, j = 1,\ldots,T$.
  • $\left(s_{c1} \nu_{j},\, s_{c1} T p_{j},\, s_{c1} \varsigma_{j}\right) \in \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{pow}}(1-\kappa)$, $\forall\, j = 1,\ldots,T$.

Where:

  • $s_{c1}$: Constraint scale of the entropy pooling optimiser. It multiplies both sides of a row, so a positive value leaves the feasible set unchanged.
  • $\boldsymbol{p}$: $T \times 1$ posterior probabilities of the observations, summing to one. They are the unknown of the entropy pooling problem.
  • $\boldsymbol{\nu}$: $T \times 1$ vector of weights that attains the risk measure, the variable of its dual representation.
  • $\boldsymbol{x}$: $T \times 1$ loss series of one asset, the negated returns, whose $j$-th entry is $x_{j}$.
  • $\alpha$: Significance level (left tail probability), $\alpha \in (0, 1)$.
  • $\kappa$: Kaniadakis deformation parameter, $\kappa \in (0, 1)$.
  • $T$: Number of observations.
  • $\ln_{\kappa}(u) = \dfrac{u^{\kappa} - u^{-\kappa}}{2 \kappa}$: Kaniadakis logarithm.
  • $\boldsymbol{\tau}$, $\boldsymbol{\varsigma}$: $T \times 1$ vectors that hold the Kaniadakis entropy budget of $\boldsymbol{\nu}$.
  • $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{re}}(1 + 2T) = \{(u,\, \boldsymbol{v},\, \boldsymbol{s}) : u \geq \sum_{j=1}^{T} s_{j} \ln(s_{j} / v_{j})\}$: The relative entropy cone.
  • $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{pow}}(\pi) = \{(a, b, c) : a^{\pi} b^{1-\pi} \geq |c|,\, a \geq 0,\, b \geq 0\}$: The power cone.

Relaxation

The encoding is not exact: the entries below bound the quantity instead of reproducing it, and the bound is tight only under the condition stated here.

The block is exact. The set that it adds is the dual description of the measure, so the largest value of the returned expression is the measure of the asset under pw.

Arguments

  • model: The entropy pooling JuMP model.
  • pw: The vector of posterior probability variables.
  • tv: The tail view constraint. It names the measure and holds its level. For the relativistic method, it also holds the deformation parameter.
  • x: The loss series of the asset.
  • sc1: The constraint scale factor.

Returns

  • expr::JuMP.AffExpr: The expression $\sum_{j=1}^{T} \nu_{j} x_{j}$.

PortfolioOptimisers.ep_var_multiplier — Function
ep_var_multiplier(x::VecNum, w::VecNum, alpha::Number)

Find the largest minimiser of the primal of the conditional value at risk of a loss series under observation probabilities.

Mathematical definition

The conditional value at risk is the minimum over $\eta$ of $\eta + \dfrac{1}{\alpha} \sum_{j=1}^{T} w_{j} (x_{j} - \eta)^{+}$. This function of $\eta$ is convex and piecewise linear, and its kinks are at the losses. Its largest minimiser is the loss at which the tail mass first reaches $\alpha$:

\[\eta^{\star} = x_{(s)}\,, \quad s = \min\left\{k : \sum_{i=1}^{k} w_{(i)} \geq \alpha\right\}\,,\]

with the losses sorted in descending order, $x_{(1)} \geq x_{(2)} \geq \ldots$.

When the tail mass of a loss is exactly $\alpha$, the minimisers form an interval. ValueatRisk takes the smallest point of the interval, so in this case the two values are adjacent losses. Every minimiser gives the same conditional value at risk, and a bound read at any of them is tight at w.

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{x}$: $T \times 1$ loss series of one asset, the negated returns, whose $j$-th entry is $x_{j}$.
  • $\boldsymbol{w}$: $T \times 1$ observation probabilities, which sum to one. In a view, they are the posterior probabilities that the model solves for.
  • $\alpha$: Significance level (left tail probability), $\alpha \in (0, 1)$.
  • $T$: Number of observations.

Arguments

  • x: The loss series (-returns).
  • w: The observation probabilities. The function normalises them to sum to one.
  • alpha: The significance level.

Returns

ep_tail_surrogate_row(tv::AbstractSequentialTailViewConstraint, x::VecNum, w::VecNum)

Read the linear upper bound of one asset's risk measure at a posterior.

Each sequential formulation bounds the measure of an asset on its primal side by an affine function of the posterior probabilities, $r_{0} + \boldsymbol{r}^{\intercal} \boldsymbol{p}$. The bound is tight at the probabilities w at which the function reads it. So the value $r_{0} + \boldsymbol{r}^{\intercal} \boldsymbol{w}$ is the measure of the asset under w.

Neither [1] nor [2] states the row. The library builds it as the tangent of the primal programme of the measure. This programme is Equation 7 of [1] for the entropic value at risk, and Equation 5 of [2] for the relativistic value at risk. The conditional value-at-risk row reads the minimisation of the conditional value at risk over its threshold, which neither work states.

Mathematical definition

The conditional value-at-risk method reads the primal at the value at risk $\eta$ of ep_var_multiplier:

\[r_{0} = \eta\,, \quad r_{j} = \dfrac{(x_{j} - \eta)^{+}}{\alpha}\,.\]

The entropic value-at-risk method reads the dual variable $z$ and the value of ep_evar, and takes the tangent of the concave primal at w:

\[r_{0} = \mathrm{EVaR}_{\alpha}(X) - z\,, \quad r_{j} = \dfrac{z e^{x_{j}/z}}{\sum_{k=1}^{T} w_{k} e^{x_{k}/z}}\,.\]

The relativistic value-at-risk method reads the pair $(t, z)$ of ep_rlvar, at which the primal is linear in the probabilities:

\[r_{0} = t + z \ln_{\kappa}\left(\dfrac{1}{\alpha T}\right)\,, \quad r_{j} = T \varphi_{\kappa}(t - x_{j},\, z)\,.\]

Where:

  • $\boldsymbol{w}$: $T \times 1$ observation probabilities, which sum to one. In a view, they are the posterior probabilities that the model solves for.
  • $\alpha$: Significance level (left tail probability), $\alpha \in (0, 1)$.
  • $\kappa$: Kaniadakis deformation parameter, $\kappa \in (0, 1)$.
  • $T$: Number of observations.
  • $\ln_{\kappa}(u) = \dfrac{u^{\kappa} - u^{-\kappa}}{2 \kappa}$: Kaniadakis logarithm.
  • $\varphi_{\kappa}(u, z)$: Smallest sum of the two tail variables that the pair of power cones of one observation allows.
  • $\mathrm{EVaR}_{\alpha}(X)$: Entropic value at risk of the loss series $\boldsymbol{x}$ at level $\alpha$.
  • $\eta$: The value at risk of the loss series under $\boldsymbol{w}$.
  • $z$: The dual variable that attains the measure under $\boldsymbol{w}$.
  • $t$: The shift that attains the relativistic value at risk under $\boldsymbol{w}$.

Arguments

  • tv: The sequential tail view constraint. It names the measure, and holds its level and the settings of its search.
  • x: The loss series of the asset (-returns).
  • w: The observation probabilities at which the function reads the bound, which sum to one.

Read the surrogate row of a sequential tail view again at a posterior, and tell whether the old row was already tight there.

  1. Return tv and true when the primal side is empty. The view is then convex, its dual blocks are exact, and no row needs a new read.

ep_evar(x::VecNum, w::VecNum, alpha::Number; args::Tuple = (),

Compute the sample entropic value at risk of a loss series and the dual variable that attains it.

ep_evar minimises the convex scalar objective of the sample EVaR with Optim.jl. The entropy pooling views need the value and the minimiser. The value compares a view with its prior, and the minimiser is the centre of the grid of GridTailView. Cajas [1] states the formula and puts the centre of the grid at the minimiser z. It does not state how to find that minimiser, so the bracket and the search are the library's own.

Mathematical definition

\[\mathrm{EVaR}_{\alpha}(X) = \min_{z > 0} \; z \ln\left(\dfrac{\sum_{j=1}^{T} w_{j} \exp(x_{j}/z)}{\alpha}\right)\,.\]

Algorithm

  1. Normalise the observation probabilities in the logarithmic domain. The result is lw.
  2. Bracket the dual variable. The upper end hi is (maximum(x) - dot(w, x)) / log(inv(alpha)). When hi is not positive, eps of its type replaces it. The lower end is hi * zlo_frac.
  3. Minimise the objective over the bracket with Optim.jl. Each evaluation uses LogExpFunctions.logsumexp, so a small z does not overflow.
  4. Return the minimum as evar, and the minimiser as z.

Arguments

  • x: The loss series (-returns).
  • w: The observation probabilities. The function normalises them to sum to one.
  • alpha: The significance level.
  • args: Additional positional arguments passed to the optimisation function. An empty args gives Optim.Brent(), which Optim.optimize selects for a bracketed scalar minimisation.
  • kwargs: Additional keyword arguments passed to the optimisation function.
  • zlo_frac: The lower end of the bracket of the dual variable, as a fraction of the upper end. nothing gives sqrt(eps(T)) for the element type T. The caller cannot state this value, because the type comes from the data. Above the upper end, (maximum(x) - dot(w, x)) / log(inv(alpha)), the objective is larger than maximum(x), which is an upper bound of the EVaR. This is a proof, so the upper end is fixed and only the lower end is a parameter.

Validation

  • 0 < zlo_frac < 1.
  • The search converges. It is a bracketed scalar minimisation of a convex function, so it fails only when args or kwargs stop it early.

ep_rlvar_tail(u::Number, z::Number, kappa::Number)

Compute the smallest tail penalty that the pair of power cones of one observation allows.

The primal programme of the relativistic value at risk has two power cones and two non-negative variables per observation. This function minimises the sum of the two variables in closed form. With this closed form, a point of the primal programme gives a row that is linear in the posterior probabilities. Cajas [2] states the cones but not the closed form, which is the library's own.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} \varphi_{\kappa}(u, z) &= \dfrac{\kappa}{1+\kappa} \left(\dfrac{2\kappa}{(1+\kappa) z}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}} \left(\dfrac{\sigma - u}{2}\right)^{\frac{1+\kappa}{\kappa}} + \kappa (1-\kappa)^{\frac{1-\kappa}{\kappa}} \left(\dfrac{z}{2\kappa}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}} \left(\dfrac{\sigma + u}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1-\kappa}{\kappa}}\,,\\ \sigma &= \sqrt{u^{2} + \dfrac{(1 - \kappa^{2}) z^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}}\,. \end{align}\]

Where:

  • $\varphi_{\kappa}(u, z)$: Smallest sum of the two tail variables that the pair of power cones of one observation allows.
  • $u$: Shifted loss of one observation, $t - x_{j}$.
  • $z > 0$: Dual variable of the primal programme.
  • $\kappa$: Kaniadakis deformation parameter, $\kappa \in (0, 1)$.
  • $\sigma$: Square root of the discriminant of the stationarity condition of $\varphi_{\kappa}$, a quadratic whose positive root $(u + \sigma) / 2$ is the minimiser.

Arguments

  • u: The shifted loss of the observation, t - x for a loss x.
  • z: The dual variable of the primal programme.
  • kappa: The deformation parameter, in (0, 1).

Returns

  • phi::Number: The smallest sum of the two tail variables of the observation.

PortfolioOptimisers.ep_row_tilt — Function
ep_row_tilt(w::VecNum, c::VecNum, b::Number; iters::Integer = 200)

Tilt a probability vector so that one linear row holds with equality, at the smallest relative entropy.

The row of a grid point is linear in the posterior probabilities. So the posterior that makes the row tight, at the smallest relative entropy from the prior, is an exponential tilt of the prior along the coefficients of the row. This tilt is the entropy pooling posterior of that one row, and it needs no solver. The tilt is the closed form of [155] for one equality constraint. Its use as the anchor of a grid is the library's own.

Mathematical definition

\[\begin{align} q_{j}(\theta) &= \dfrac{w_{j} e^{-\theta c_{j}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{T} w_{i} e^{-\theta c_{i}}}\,,\\ \sum_{j=1}^{T} q_{j}(\theta) c_{j} &= b\,. \end{align}\]

The value of the row under the tilt decreases strictly as $\theta$ increases, from $\max_{j} c_{j}$ to $\min_{j} c_{j}$ over the observations with $w_{j} > 0$. So the tilt exists if and only if $b$ is strictly inside that range. A tilt cannot put mass on an observation of zero prior probability, so the coefficient of such an observation sets no bound.

Arguments

  • w: The prior probabilities, which sum to one.
  • c: The coefficients of the row.
  • b: The value that the row must take.
  • iters::Integer = 200: The largest number of bisection steps. The bisection also stops when the midpoint does not change. For Float64 this happens near step 64, so the limit applies only to a number type of higher precision.

Validation

  • iters >= 1.

Returns

  • q::Option{VecNum}: The tilted probabilities. The function returns nothing when no tilt attains b, because b is outside the range of c over the support of w. It also returns nothing when the tilted row is not finite.

Algorithm

  1. Return nothing when b is outside the open range of c over the observations with w > 0.
  2. Bracket the root. Double the tilt away from zero until the value of the row crosses b.
  3. Bisect the bracket until the midpoint does not change, or for iters steps if that comes first.
  4. Return nothing when the sum of the tilted weights is zero or not a number. Otherwise return the normalised weights.

  3. Span the dual variable from zc * (1 - pct) to zc * (1 + pct) in K points. K is odd, so the centre is a point of the grid, and a grid of one point is the centre alone.

Find the primal point of the relativistic value at risk that a posterior attains when it meets an upper-bound view.

A grid point states the view as one row. A posterior that makes the row tight reaches the target only if that grid point is the point that the posterior attains. ep_rlvar_anchor finds the pair that meets both conditions, and the grid of GridTailView has this pair as its centre. The function calls no solver. Cajas [2] states no grid and no anchor, so both are the library's own, and both use the primal programme.

Arguments

  • x: The loss series (-returns).
  • w: The prior probabilities, which sum to one.
  • alpha: The significance level.
  • kappa: The deformation parameter, in (0, 1).
  • rhs: The target relativistic value at risk.
  • t: The shift variable at the start of the iteration.
  • z: The dual variable at the start of the iteration.
  • iters::Integer = 50: The largest number of steps of the iteration.
  • tol::Number = 1e-10: The relative distance from the target at which the iteration stops.
  • tilt_iters::Integer = 200: The largest number of bisection steps of the tilt of one row, in ep_row_tilt.
  • args: Additional positional arguments passed to the optimisation function.
  • kwargs: Additional keyword arguments passed to the optimisation function.
  • bracket: The spans of the searches, which go to ep_rlvar and ep_rlvar_shift.

Returns

  • res::Option{@NamedTuple{t::Number, z::Number, w::VecNum}}: The pair and the posterior that attains it, or nothing when the iteration does not reach the target.

Algorithm

  1. Build the row of the current pair with ep_rlvar_grid_row. Return nothing when the row is not finite.
  2. Tilt the prior with ep_row_tilt, so that the row is tight. Return nothing when no probability vector makes the row tight.
  3. Compute the pair again with ep_rlvar, as the minimiser at the tilted probabilities.
  4. Stop when the relativistic value at risk of the tilted probabilities is within tol of the target. Return the pair and those probabilities.
  5. Return nothing if iters steps do not reach the target.

Build the grid of primal points a relativistic value-at-risk view is written on.

Cajas [2] states the primal programme and no grid of its points. The grid, its translation and its anchor are the library's own, and they use the primal programme.

For a view with an upper-bound half, the centre of the grid is the pair that ep_rlvar_anchor finds. Each shift then minimises the objective at the posterior of that pair. For a lower-bound view, and for a view whose anchor does not converge, the centre is the dual variable of the prior. Each shift then minimises the objective under the prior probabilities, minus the distance from the prior value to the target. The relativistic value at risk and the shift that attains it are both translation-equivariant, so a posterior that moves the value to the target acts, to first order, like a translation of each loss by that distance.

Arguments

  • x: The loss series (-returns).
  • w: The prior probabilities, which sum to one.
  • alpha: The significance level.
  • kappa: The deformation parameter, in (0, 1).
  • op: The comparison operator of the view.
  • rhs: The target relativistic value at risk.
  • zstar: The dual variable that attains the prior RLVaR of the asset.
  • pv: The prior RLVaR of the asset.
  • pct: The half-width of the grid, as a fraction of the dual variable at its centre.
  • K: The number of grid points.
  • iters::Integer = 50: The largest number of steps of the anchor.
  • tol::Number = 1e-10: The relative distance from the target at which the anchor stops.
  • tilt_iters::Integer = 200: The largest number of bisection steps of the tilt of one row, in ep_row_tilt.
  • args: Additional positional arguments passed to the optimisation function.
  • kwargs: Additional keyword arguments passed to the optimisation function.
  • bracket: The spans of the searches, which go to ep_rlvar and ep_rlvar_shift.

Returns

  • t::VecNum: The shift variable of each grid point.
  • z::VecNum: The dual variable of each grid point.

Algorithm

  1. Use the pair of the prior as the centre. Use the distance from the prior value to the target as the translation of each shift.
  2. If the view has an upper-bound half, use the pair and the posterior of ep_rlvar_anchor instead, with no translation. Keep the pair of the prior if the anchor does not converge.
  3. Span the dual variable from zc * (1 - pct) to zc * (1 + pct) in K points. K is odd, so the centre is a point of the grid, and a grid of one point is the centre alone.

PortfolioOptimisers.ep_sequential_start — Method
ep_sequential_start(tv::AbstractSequentialTailViewConstraint, w::VecNum)

Read the first surrogate row of a sequential tail view at probabilities under which the row can meet the view.

A linear upper bound is tight where the function reads it. But the value of the row cannot fall below the smallest value of the row over the simplex. If the target is further below the prior than that, the first solve has no feasible point.

This happens with the relativistic value at risk. At the pair of the prior, its bound has a floor at the shift plus the deformed logarithm, and only the tail term above the floor can change. ep_sequential_start moves the multipliers toward the target with a chain of exponential tilts before a solver runs. The anchors of GridTailView use the same method to find the centres of their grids. The function calls no solver.

Algorithm

  1. Normalise w to sum to one, and read the row at w with ep_refine_tail_view. Return tv when its primal side is empty, because the view is then convex and the row is empty.
  2. Calculate need, the value that the row must reach. It is the target, minus the constant of the row, minus the coefficient-weighted sum of the measures of the dual side under the current probabilities. ep_tail_surrogate_row gives each measure.
  3. Return tv when the largest coefficient of the row is at least need. A probability vector then meets the row, and the solve can start.
  4. Otherwise, tilt the probabilities with ep_row_tilt, so that the row goes nine tenths of the way from its current value to its largest coefficient. Read the row again at the tilted probabilities, and go back to step 2.
  5. Do at most iters steps. When the tilt does not exist, return tv with its last row.

Arguments

  • tv: The sequential tail view constraint, with any row.
  • w: The prior probability weights.

Returns

  • tv::AbstractSequentialTailViewConstraint: The tail view constraint with its first row.

PortfolioOptimisers.add_ep_tail_view! — Function
add_ep_tail_view!(model::JuMP.Model, pw, tv::AbstractEntropyPoolingTailView,
                  sc1::Number)

Add the variables and constraints of one tail view to an entropy pooling JuMP model.

Every conditional, entropic and relativistic value-at-risk view goes into the model through add_ep_tail_view!. Each formulation has its own method, which dispatches on the tail view constraint that ep_tail_views! made. The three dual tail view constraints share one method, and the three sequential tail view constraints share another. Both methods write one dual block for each asset with ep_tail_dual_block!, and they differ in the row that bounds the sum. Cajas [1] and Cajas [2] state the dual, the integer and the entropic grid formulations. The sequential formulation and the grid formulation of the relativistic value at risk are the library's own.

JuMP formulation

This section covers the five methods and every entry that each method registers. No entry has a name. One model holds one block for each view, so a name collides at the second view of a family. Nothing reads these entries back by name.

Variables

  • pw: $\boldsymbol{p}$: $T \times 1$ posterior probabilities of the observations, summing to one. They are the unknown of the entropy pooling problem. The caller gives it, and every entry below reads it.
  • nu, tau, varsigma: ep_tail_dual_block! makes them once for each asset of a dual tail view constraint, and once for each asset of the dual side of a sequential tail view constraint. Its # JuMP formulation section names them.
  • y, q: $\boldsymbol{y}$ and $\boldsymbol{q}$, two $\bar{s} \times 1$ vectors that the IntegerConditionalValueatRiskViewConstraint method makes once for each asset. y is binary, and q has a lower bound of zero.
  • y: $\boldsymbol{y}$, a binary $K \times 1$ vector that the GridEntropicValueatRiskViewConstraint and GridRelativisticValueatRiskViewConstraint methods make. It selects the grid point at which the posterior meets the view.

Expressions

  • $\varepsilon$: The left hand side of the view, which each method builds once and which has no name. For the dual tail view constraints, it is the coefficient-weighted sum of the expressions that ep_tail_dual_block! returns for each asset. For the integer tail view constraint, it is the coefficient-weighted sum of the tail sums of each asset. For the sequential tail view constraints, it is the coefficient-weighted sum of the expressions of the dual side, plus the surrogate row $b + \boldsymbol{c}^{\intercal} \boldsymbol{p}$.

Constraints

The method of the three dual tail view constraints, LinearConditionalValueatRiskViewConstraint, ConicEntropicValueatRiskViewConstraint and ConicRelativisticValueatRiskViewConstraint, registers one block of ep_tail_dual_block! for each asset that the view names, and one row:

  • $s_{c1} \left(\bar{c} - \sum_{i} \gamma_{i} \sum_{j=1}^{T} \nu_{i,\,j} x_{i,\,j}\right) \leq 0$.

The method of the three sequential tail view constraints, SequentialConditionalValueatRiskViewConstraint, SequentialEntropicValueatRiskViewConstraint and SequentialRelativisticValueatRiskViewConstraint, registers one block of ep_tail_dual_block! for each asset of the dual side, and one row over both sides. When the largest absolute coefficient of the surrogate row is larger than one, the method divides the row by it. So the coefficients of the surrogate row stay in $[-1, 1]$, at a small dual variable of a relativistic value at risk too:

  • $s_{c1} \left(\bar{c} - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \gamma_{i} \sum_{j=1}^{T} \nu_{i,\,j} x_{i,\,j} - b - \sum_{j=1}^{T} c_{j} p_{j}\right) \Big/ \max\left(1, \lVert \boldsymbol{c} \rVert_{\infty}\right) \leq 0$.

The IntegerConditionalValueatRiskViewConstraint method registers five rows for each asset that the view names, over the window of the $\bar{s}$ largest losses of that asset:

  • $s_{c1} \left(q_{j} - y_{j}\right) \leq 0$, $\forall\, j = 1,\ldots,\bar{s}$.
  • $s_{c1} \left(q_{j} - p_{[j]}\right) \leq 0$, $\forall\, j = 1,\ldots,\bar{s}$.
  • $s_{c1} \left(p_{[j]} - (1 - y_{j-1}) - q_{j}\right) \leq 0$, $\forall\, j = 2,\ldots,\bar{s}$.
  • $s_{c1} \left(y_{j} - y_{j+1}\right) \leq 0$, $\forall\, j = 1,\ldots,\bar{s}-1$.
  • $s_{c1} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{s}} q_{j} - \alpha\right) = 0$.

The method also registers one row on $\varepsilon = \sum_{i} \dfrac{\gamma_{i}}{\alpha} \sum_{j=1}^{\bar{s}} q_{i,\,j} x_{i,\,[j]}$. The operator of the view selects one of these three rows:

  • $s_{c1} \left(\varepsilon - \bar{c}\right) = 0$ under :eq.
  • $s_{c1} \left(\bar{c} - \varepsilon\right) \leq 0$ under :geq.
  • $s_{c1} \left(\varepsilon - \bar{c}\right) \leq 0$ under :leq.

The GridEntropicValueatRiskViewConstraint and GridRelativisticValueatRiskViewConstraint methods each register the rows below. Each method divides the row of each grid point by its bound:

  • $s_{c1} \left(\sum_{k=1}^{K} y_{k} - 1\right) = 0$.
  • $s_{c1} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{T} \dfrac{c_{k,\,j}}{b_{k}} p_{j} - 1 - M M_{k} (1 - y_{k})\right) \leq 0$, $\forall\, k = 1,\ldots,K$, with $M_{k} = \max_{j} c_{k,\,j} / b_{k} - 1$.

Where:

  • $s_{c1}$: Constraint scale of the entropy pooling optimiser. It multiplies both sides of a row, so a positive value leaves the feasible set unchanged.
  • $\boldsymbol{p}$: $T \times 1$ posterior probabilities of the observations, summing to one. They are the unknown of the entropy pooling problem.
  • $\alpha$: Significance level (left tail probability), $\alpha \in (0, 1)$.
  • $T$: Number of observations.
  • $\bar{c}$: Target conditional value at risk of the view.
  • $x_{i,\,j}$: The loss of asset $i$ at observation $j$, which is the negated return.
  • $\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i}$: The $T \times 1$ vector of weights that attains the measure of asset $i$, from its block of ep_tail_dual_block!.
  • $\bar{c}$: The target of the view, for each of the three measures. A sequential tail view constraint holds the view as a lower bound, so its target has the sign of that orientation.
  • $\bar{s}$: The length of the window of the largest losses of one asset, from ep_sbar.
  • $x_{[j]}$, $p_{[j]}$: The loss and the posterior probability of the observation at position $j$ of that window, which is in ascending order.
  • $\gamma_{i}$: The coefficient that the view gives asset $i$.
  • $\mathcal{P}$: The assets on the dual side of a sequential tail view constraint.
  • $b$, $\boldsymbol{c}$: The surrogate row that a sequential tail view constraint holds, from ep_tail_surrogate_row.
  • $\boldsymbol{y}$: A binary vector. In the integer conditional value-at-risk method, it marks the tail of the window of one asset. In the two grid methods, it selects one grid point.
  • $\boldsymbol{q}$: The $\bar{s} \times 1$ vector that holds the tail mass of each observation of the window. The tail mass is $p_{[j]}$ above the lowest marked observation, a part of $p_{[j]}$ at that observation, and zero below it.
  • $\varepsilon$: The left hand side of an integer conditional value-at-risk view, which is the coefficient-weighted sum of the posterior CVaRs of the assets.
  • $K$: The number of grid points that the tail view constraint holds.
  • $c_{k,\,j}$: The scaled coefficient of observation $j$ at grid point $k$, from ep_evar_grid_row or ep_rlvar_grid_row.
  • $b_{k}$: The scaled bound of grid point $k$, from the same two functions. For the entropic value at risk, it is $\alpha$ times the reciprocal that ep_evar_grid_row returns. For the relativistic value at risk, it is the target that ep_rlvar_grid_row returns.
  • $M_{k}$: The smallest big-M constant that releases the row of grid point $k$.
  • $M$: The big-M multiplier that the grid tail view constraint holds.

Relaxation

The encoding is not exact: the entries below bound the quantity instead of reproducing it, and the bound is tight only under the condition stated here.

The two grid methods and the sequential method bound the statistic. The dual method is exact. The integer method is exact for each posterior whose tail of mass $\alpha$ is inside the window of the $\bar{s}$ largest losses. When $\bar{s} = T$, this holds for every posterior.

The sequential method is a restriction of the view, and each re-solve makes it tighter.

  1. Direction. The surrogate row bounds each measure of the primal side from above, and each of these measures has a negative coefficient. So the value of the row is at or below the left hand side of the view. A posterior that meets the row meets the view.

  2. Quantity. The coefficient-weighted sum of the posterior measures that the view names, which is a statistic of pw.

  3. Tightness. The row is tight at the posterior at which the function read it. entropy_pooling reads the row again at each posterior with ep_refine_tail_view, and solves again. It stops when the slack is within the tol of the tail view constraint, or after iters re-solves. At the fixed point the posterior meets the view to that tolerance. Before the fixed point, the posterior meets the view with slack.

The two grid methods are also a restriction of the view.

  1. Direction. Every grid point is a feasible point of the primal programme of the statistic, so its row bounds the statistic from above. The block asks one grid point to hold, so the posterior statistic is at or below the target. The encoding can only be tighter than the view, so a posterior that meets the block meets the view.

  2. Quantity. The posterior entropic value at risk under the GridEntropicValueatRiskViewConstraint method, and the posterior relativistic value at risk under the GridRelativisticValueatRiskViewConstraint method. Both are statistics of pw.

  3. Tightness. The bound is tight when the grid holds the point that the posterior attains. ep_evar_anchor and ep_rlvar_anchor put the centre of the grid at that point. When the anchor does not converge, the grid uses the point of the prior as its centre, and the posterior statistic can be strictly below the target. In that case, increase pct or K.

Each method divides each grid row by its bound, so that the right hand side is one. At a small dual variable, the bound $b_{k}$ is far below the feasibility tolerance of a solver. At that scale the whole row is inside the tolerance, and the solver accepts any posterior on it. The selector can then select that grid point when the posterior does not meet the view.

$M M_{k}$ releases the rows of the grid points that the selector does not select. $\boldsymbol{p}$ sums to one, so the left hand side of row $k$ is never larger than $\max_{j} c_{k,\,j} / b_{k}$, and $M_{k}$ is the smallest constant that releases the row. An $M$ below one can remove a posterior that the view accepts, so $M \geq 1$. An $M$ above one gives the released rows a margin for a posterior whose sum is one only to the tolerance of the solver. But it also increases the slack that the integrality tolerance opens on the selected row, so the default is $M = 1$.

The lower-bound half of a grid view is a relaxation, and this function does not receive it. The view needs the primal objective to be at least the target at every dual variable, and the rows ask for this only at the $K$ grid points. So the posterior statistic, which is the minimum over all dual variables, can be below the target. ep_add_evar_view! and ep_add_rlvar_view! add those rows to the entropy pooling constraint dictionary, not to the model.

  • pw: The vector of posterior probability variables.
  • tv: The tail view constraint.
  • sc1: The constraint scale factor.
