## Summary

- **Twenty-five rows plus two verified**, in four sections. A: nine rows of the price-level
  tracking and reversion school since the survey (2 read or restated with equations, 7 abstract
  only). B: nine rows of exponentiated-gradient descendants, ensembles and pattern matching (1 read
  — a precursor added for the objective it carries — 8 abstract only, one of them also restated).
  C: seven rows of the online-convex-optimisation reading, all **read**, plus three tables: two
  further mirror maps, nine learning-rate schedules, and five regret comparators. D: the two rows
  the 1149 ledger flagged as unverified, both now **read** and corrected.
- **Three rows are already expressed by the roster, formula for formula** (section C):
  `GradientProjection(; eta)` under `EuclideanProjection` on the default set **is** Zinkevich's
  fixed-rate online gradient descent — and more than the 1997 paper's GP, whose non-negativity
  is assumed, not enforced; `ExpectationMaximisation(; eta)` with `eta in (0, 1)` **is** Soft-Bayes,
  so the EM row inherits an `O(sqrt(T N log N))` bound with **no lower bound on the price
  relatives**; and the expert-mixture rows of the dynamic-regret family (Ader, `Sword_small`) are
  `ExpertMixture` over `GradientProjection` at a rate grid under `ExponentiatedGradient` as the
  weighting, up to a shared-gradient mode and non-uniform initial weights.
- **One set-2 row is a defect as transcribed** (section D): the survey's switching-portfolio
  update is exact only when its `w_t` is the price-adjusted holding; applied to the held `w_t` the
  leaf `SwitchingPortfolio(; gamma)` of ADR 0156 never reads `x_t` and glides deterministically to
  uniform. The paper's rule is an `ExpertMixture` over single-asset constant rebalanced portfolios
  under a NEW fixed-share weighting, `SwitchingWeighting(; gamma = 1/3)`, with `SwitchingPortfolio`
  a constructor of it, as `UniversalPortfolio` is. The other verified row, OLMAR-2, stands: the
  `ExponentialMovingAverage` recursion of ADR 0158 is the paper's Eq. 2 verbatim, but the paper
  states **no `alpha` default** — 0.5 is a mid-plateau choice — and is one step ambiguous about its
  own seed.
- **What is new, grouped by where it lands.** *Forecast statistics* (`AbstractPriceLevelStatistic`,
  the `me` slot): a per-asset reweighted-relative recursion (RPRT), a Gaussian-weighted average
  (GWR), a linear-trend extrapolation and a slope switch between two statistics (LOAD, TPPT), a
  radial-basis composite over statistics with a performance-chosen centre (AICTR), a blend of two
  statistics, a three-state pattern switch (KTPT, unread), a random-subspace ensemble forecast
  (PIRA, unread). *Rule state or slots*: a momentum carrier on `ExponentiatedGradient` (EGM), an
  `alpha` uniform-mix field on `ExponentiatedGradient` whose state must carry the unmixed `w_t`
  (EG(α, η)), a diagonal scale on `ForecastReversion`'s direction (RPRT), a rate schedule slot
  (`WindowedBestRate`, the anytime `eta_t`, the doubling trick, the self-confident and residual-driven
  rates), a `predictor` slot and a secondary iterate for the optimistic step, an `N`-vector carrier
  and a `DiagonalProjection` for AdaGrad, an extra-gradient rule. *Mixture weightings*:
  `WeakAggregatingAlgorithm(; c)`, `SwitchingWeighting(; gamma)`, a shared-gradient mode, a
  sample-return weighting. *Selectors*: `ClusterMatch` over window vectors. *Objective terms on a
  held optimiser*: a rank-one uncentred covariance estimator (SPOLC), a regime-switched signed beta
  term (DRICORN-K), a log-barrier regulariser. *Geometries*: `TsallisProjection(; alpha)` and
  `LogBarrierProjection`, both scalar roots on the default set; `BarrierGramProjection` needs a
  solver. *Evaluation*: dynamic-regret comparators (be-the-leader, path-length, per-period best,
  `K`-switch) and the path length beside the number.
- **Not expressible on the current head, by input or by design**: a rule reading trading volume
  (MRvol) or an external market index (DRICORN-K) needs a side panel the head does not carry; the
  distributed network rule (DMR), the barrier-regularised Newton step's restart schedule
  (Ada-BARRONS) and the per-asset kernel weighting of KTPT are new mechanisms whose full texts are
  closed or whose design is a research code.
- **The comparator fact for the evaluation surface** (§C.4): `log_wealth_regret(a, b)` with `b`
  the follow-the-leader rule run through the head is regret against a *member of the family*, not
  the literature's prefix comparator, which is be-the-leader — the leader fit through row `t` and
  scored on it — and which beats BCRP by the FTL–BTL lemma. Every dynamic-regret notion is a
  comparator *sequence*; the verb already accepts any `b`, and what is missing is a comparator that
  is not one estimator's causal prediction, plus the path length reported beside the number.

## A. Price-level tracking and reversion since the survey (the peak-tracking school)

Nine papers, one school: every row reads a **Price Relative Forecast** `xhat` off a window of
price levels (or a recursion on price relatives) and takes one of two closed-form steps that the
library already names. The **tracking step** is Lai, Dai, Ren and Huang's (2018, IEEE TNNLS
29(7), DOI 10.1109/TNNLS.2017.2705658, **read** — §III, Algorithm 1 and §III-D, pages 3–6 of the
maintainer's copy on disk) Algorithm 1: `max_b <b, xhat> s.t. ||b - w_t|| <= eps`, solved as `xhat_perp = (I - 11'/N) xhat`,
`w_{t+1} = Proj_Delta(w_t + eps xhat_perp / ||xhat_perp||)` (hold when `xhat_perp = 0`), `w = 5`,
`eps = 100`; `eps` is a step **length**, and at the default the output is one-hot on the argmax of
`xhat` (the ticket #1158 finding). That is `PeakPriceTracking(; me = WindowPeak(5), eps = 100)`.
The **reversion step** is OLMAR's passive-aggressive step, `ForecastReversion(; me, eps)`. The
school's contribution is almost entirely in **what `xhat` is**, so most rows below are "a new
`AbstractPriceLevelStatistic` in the `me` slot of an existing rule", and the section names the
statistic each time. Two rows (SPOLC, PIRA) add a covariance-like term instead and belong to the
solved family.

### Note on constructors

The tracking rows share one closed form with the peak-price-tracking paper: a fixed-length step
`eps xhat_perp / ||xhat_perp||` from `w_t`, then `Proj_Delta`. AICTR (JMLR Eq. 13, read) and, by
their abstracts and the later literature, KTPT and TPPT change only `xhat` (and KTPT adds a
per-asset weight). By ADR 0158's own rule for the reversion step — one mechanism-named struct,
paper names as constructors — the tracking step wants the same shape: a mechanism-named rule
(`ForecastTracking(; me, eps)`, the step that maximises the increasing factor within an
`eps`-ball) with `PeakPriceTracking(; window = 5, eps = 100)` the constructor filling
`me = WindowPeak(5)`, and AICTR/TPPT constructors filling their composite statistics with
`eps = 1000`/unread. The roster's `PeakPriceTracking(; me = WindowPeak, eps)` already carries
the `me` slot, so this is a rename, not a redesign; the ticket should say which name is the
struct. RPRT, GWR and LOAD are the reversion step (`ForecastReversion`) with new statistics, and
RPRT alone adds a term the slot cannot carry (the diagonal scale). SPOLC and PIRA are the solved
family with a covariance-like term that is **not a Prior's `sigma` as estimated today**: SPOLC's
rank-one estimate is fully specified above and is a small new covariance estimator; PIRA's
predictor is unread.

Three findings the brief's questions did not anticipate, stated up front because the rows depend
on them:

1. **GP is OGD.** Helmbold and co-authors' (1997) gradient-projection update, Eq. 5,
   `w_{t+1} = w_t + eta (∇L - mean(∇L) 1)`, is the Euclidean projection of `w_t + eta ∇L` onto the
   *hyperplane* `1'w = 1` and nothing else; their Theorem 3 *assumes* the iterates stay
   non-negative. Zinkevich's OGD projects onto the whole simplex. The two coincide whenever no
   coordinate would go negative: `Proj_Delta(y) = max(y - theta, 0)` with `theta` fixing the
   budget, and when nothing is clipped `theta = mean(y) - 1/N = eta mean(∇L)`. So
   `GradientProjection(; eta)` under `EuclideanProjection` on `BoundedAllocationSet()` **is**
   Zinkevich's fixed-rate OGD exactly, and is *more* than the 1997 paper's GP, whose non-negativity
   is assumed rather than enforced.
2. **Soft-Bayes is the 1997 EM update, formula for formula.** Helmbold and co-authors' Eq. 7,
   `w_{t+1,i} = w_{t,i} (eta (∇L_i - 1) + 1)` with `∇L_i = x_{t,i} / <w_t, x_t>`, and Orseau and
   co-authors' Eq. 7, `w_{t+1,i} = w_{t,i} (1 - eta + eta p_{t,i} / M_t)` with `M_t = <w_t, p_t>`,
   are the same update; the 2017 paper cites the 1998 EG paper but not the 1997 one. What is new
   in 2017 is the *analysis* (no lower bound on `x`) and the online correction.
3. **AdaGrad's diagonal projection on the simplex is a scalar root, not a quadratic programme.**
   Duchi and co-authors' §5.2 reduces the diagonal-Mahalanobis projection onto an `l1` ball to a
   continuous quadratic knapsack solved by one sort; on the simplex it is
   `w_i = max(0, y_i - theta / H_{t,ii})` with `theta` the root of `sum_i max(0, y_i - theta / H_{t,ii}) = 1`.
   A `DiagonalProjection` geometry therefore costs what `EuclideanProjection` costs. The
   full-matrix version is the quadratic programme `GramProjection` already pays.

### C.2 Mirror maps beyond entropy and Euclidean, as candidate Projection Geometries

The library's three geometries are the Euclidean norm, relative entropy and the Gram norm (ADR
0159). The OCO literature runs the portfolio, or its expert-simplex twin the bandit, under three
more. For each: the potential `Ψ`, its Bregman divergence `D_Ψ(u, w) = Ψ(u) - Ψ(w) - <∇Ψ(w), u - w>`,
what the constrained mirror step `w_{t+1} = argmin_{Delta} eta <w, g_t> + D_Ψ(w, w_t)` costs on the
simplex, and which portfolio paper used it. The stationarity condition of that step is
`∇Ψ(w_{t+1}) = ∇Ψ(w_t) - eta g_t - lambda 1` with `lambda` the budget multiplier, and the
non-negativity is automatic wherever `∇Ψ` blows up at zero (all three below).

### C.4 Dynamic-regret comparators, as one row

`R_T(u_{1:T}) = sum_t f_t(w_t) - sum_t f_t(u_t) = sum_t log <u_t, x_t> - sum_t log <w_t, x_t>`. The
comparator sequence is the whole difference between the notions below; the strategy side is
always the causal `w_t`.

**What the literature means precisely, on the brief's question.** "Regret against the hindsight
prefix" is the be-the-leader sequence: `u*_t` scored on the row it was fit through. Its wealth is
not causal by one row, and by the FTL–BTL lemma it is at least the BCRP's wealth. A prefix
comparator computed *causally* (`u*_{t-1}` on `x_t`) is the follow-the-leader strategy, a rule of
the family, and its regret is the ordinary static regret of FTL — `O(K^2 log T)` in the ledger's
FTL row — with the sign reversed when it is placed on the comparator side of the verb. The two
differ by exactly `sum_t [log <u*_t, x_t> - log <u*_{t-1}, x_t>]`, the FTL "stability" sum that
Hazan's Lemma 5.3 bounds. So `log_wealth_regret(a, b)` with `b = FollowTheLeader(; sel = Prefix())`
through the head is regret against FTL, not a dynamic regret, and the library should say so where
it documents the verb.

### D.1 Switching portfolios, SP

**What the existing row said.** Section 2.2 gave the fixed-`gamma` form as
`w_{t+1} = (1 - gamma - gamma/(N - 1)) w_t + gamma/(N - 1)` (Singer's Eq. 6), state "the posterior

**What the paper says.** Eq. 6 is exact, but its `w^i_t` is defined one line above it as
`w^i_t = S^i_t / sum_j S^j_t`, where `S^i_t` is the **worth of asset `i` after trading day `t`**
(Eq. 4: `S^i_{t+1} = [(1 - gamma) S^i_t + gamma/(N-1) sum_{j != i} S^j_t] x^i_{t+1}`; Eq. 5 the
same with the sum over all `j`). So the `w_t` on the right of Eq. 6 is the *post-return* fraction
— the ledger's `what_t`, not the held `w_t` — and the wealth step `S^i_t <- S^i_t x^i_t` is inside
the symbol. The paper's algorithm is a Bayesian mixture over switching regimes of **basic
strategies** (§3: "pure" single-asset strategies throughout, with any strategy admitted — §4
feeds the universal portfolio in as a basic strategy at `O(t^3)`). Per period it is (i) the wealth
step, each strategy's posterior weight multiplied by its realised return, then (ii) the switching
mix, keep with probability `1 - gamma`, redistribute uniformly to the other `N - 1` with
probability `gamma`, then (iii) the portfolio is the mixture of the strategies' next-period
portfolios. The paper itself notes the update "resembles the fixed-share weight update of
Herbster and Warmuth (1995)". The **second version** lets `gamma` vary with the run length,
`gammahat(Delta t) = (1/2) / (Delta t + 1)` (the Krichevsky–Trofimov weighting), and then carries
one worth per (asset, start day) pair, Eq. 7 `S^i_{t+1,t0} = ((t - t0 + 1/2)/(t - t0 + 1)) S^i_{t,t0} x^i_{t+1}`
and Eq. 8 `S^i_{t+1,t+1} = (sum_{j != i} sum_{t0=1}^{t} S^j_{t,t0} / (2 (t - t0 + 1))) x^i_{t+1}`,

update that directly manipulates a portfolio weight vector". `gamma = 1/3` in every experiment
of Table 1 ("a reasonable time to hold a single stock is typically a few days"; the expected
holding time is `1/gamma`). Theorem 2 is the **varying-`gamma`** bound; the fixed-`gamma` bound is
the unnumbered display at the end of §3.

price-adjusted holding. A leaf rule that applies Eq. 6 to the *held* `w_t` never reads `x_t`: its

not a portfolio rule at all — that is the defect in the set-2 leaf `SwitchingPortfolio(; gamma)`
