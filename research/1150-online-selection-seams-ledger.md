# The seams an online-selection estimator consumes: a ledger

Research ticket #1150 of map #1148. Measured against the worktree at `63286db307` (one docstring-only commit behind `dev` at `4c070a9792`) on 2026-09-18. Every line number is a claim about that commit.

The family in question is the one `research/prototypes/09_online_portfolio_selection.jl` prototypes: a per-period update `(state, w_t, x_t) -> (state', w_{t+1})` over a price-relative row `x_t = 1 .+ X[t, :]`, a simplex projection, the previous portfolio `w_t`, and for two of the seven algorithms a forecast `xhat_{t+1}` formed from reconstructed price levels. Its read-out is its own state: the weights it holds *are* the answer, and no batch fit over a window produces them.

The question is what each of the library's eight seams hands such an estimator, and where each one assumes the read-out is a batch fit.

## Summary

| # | Seam | Kept or broken | The one line |
|---|------|----------------|--------------|
| 1 | Online arm of the fold loop | **Broken at the read-out; kept at the loop** | `thread_online_folds!` threads any `partial_fit!`-able estimator, but `optimise(opt)` reconstitutes a carrier and runs the batch path (ADR 0137); the family writes its own `online_readout`/`_optimise` pair. The loop hands `w_prev` to the *per-fold copy after* the fold, never to the state before it. |
| 2 | Batch entry | **Kept** | `optimise(opt, rd)` refuses only an `Online` wrapper; nothing forbids a `_optimise(opt, rd)` that processes the window causally and returns the last allocation as a `NaiveOptimisationResult`. |
| 3 | Previous weights | **Kept, but arrives too late for the recursion** | `previous_weights` gives `prev.res.w` or `prev.hw.w`; it reaches the estimator through `factory(esti, w_prev)` on the per-fold copy, only when `needs_previous_weights(est)`. The state must carry `w_t` itself. |
| 4 | Weight drift and fees | **Kept for a one-row test window; a multi-row fold holds one target and drifts it** | `calc_net_returns` and `held_weights_result` drift the fold's *one* target `w0`; the weight path is rebuilt from it. Fees come from `res.fees`, which a `NaiveOptimisationResult` does not carry. |
| 5 | Constraints on a solver-free head | **Kept for bounds only; the finaliser is not the simplex projection** | `opt_weight_bounds` preserves the *input's* sum and short-circuits when bounds already hold; every other constraint needs a JuMP model with a `w` variable. |
| 6 | A prior that folds | **Kept, unused by five of seven algorithms** | `Online(pe)` seeds a refit buffer; `EmpiricalPrior` folds exactly; no expected-returns estimator reads price levels, so OLMAR's moving average and RMR's spatial median are not among them. `LowOrderPrior` requires `mu` and `sigma`. |
| 7 | Search and scoring | **Kept** | `"field.path" => values` becomes an Accessors lens; a candidate is scored by `expected_risk(r, pred)` over the net series; `MeanReturn(; flag = true)` is log wealth per period, so the window is searchable today. Regret against a second run has no home in the search. |
| 8 | Hindsight benchmark | **Confirmed** | `MeanRisk(; obj = MaximumReturn(), opt = JuMPOptimiser(; ret = LogarithmicReturn()))` reproduces `best_crp` to `1.8e-12` in weights at `1e-12` Clarabel tolerances, `4.9e-8` at defaults. |

**Where the loop assumes a batch read-out.** Exactly one place: `optimise(opt)` at `09_OnlineOptimisation.jl:670`, and the `online_readout` methods behind it, which are per-host and do not reach an unknown type. The loop itself (`thread_online_folds!`) assumes nothing about the read-out beyond "returns a `NonFiniteAllocationOptimisationResult` that `predict` can score". The identity ADR 0137 promises, `optimise(opt) == optimise(opt, rd[1:t])`, is the one this family cannot keep: after `t` rows its read-out is `w_{t+1}`, and its batch verb over `rd[1:t]` is the *same* causal pass, so the identity holds trivially only if the family defines its batch verb as that pass (seam 2).

## 8. The hindsight benchmark

`LogarithmicReturn` (`05_JuMP/02_JuMPConstraints/02_Returns_and_ObjectiveFunctions.jl:486-503`) builds `mean(t)` with `[t_j, k, k + X_j w] in ExponentialCone` per observation (`:1968-1994`), so under `MaximumReturn`, `WeightBounds(0, 1)` and `bgt = 1` the programme is `max (1/T) Σ_t log(1 + x_tᵀ w)` on the simplex, which is `best_crp`'s objective divided by `T`.

Measured in a bare process, `julia --project=test -t 1 tmp/bcrp_check_1150.jl`, `BLAS.set_num_threads(1)`, on two `60 × 4` `StableRNGs` fixtures, `best_crp(; iters = 500_000, tol = 1e-15)` against `MeanRisk(; obj = MaximumReturn(), opt = JuMPOptimiser(; pe = EmpiricalPrior(), slv, ret = LogarithmicReturn(), wb = WeightBounds(; lb = 0, ub = 1), bgt = 1))` with Clarabel:

| Fixture | Optimum | Clarabel tolerances | `‖w - w_bcrp‖_∞` | `‖w - w_bcrp‖_2` | `log S(bcrp) - log S(MeanRisk)` |
|---------|---------|---------------------|------------------|------------------|--------------------------------|
| 1: independent noise, distinct drifts (`StableRNG(1150)`) | corner, one asset | `1e-12` (`tol_gap_abs`, `tol_gap_rel`, `tol_feas`, `max_iter = 500`) | `3.2e-10` | `4.5e-10` | `-1.6e-11` |
| 1 | corner | defaults | `5.6e-8` | `7.0e-8` | `4.2e-9` |
| 2: volatility pumping, anti-phase `±30 %` swings plus `1 %` log-normal noise (`StableRNG(2150)`) | interior, `w = [0.4864, 0.5136, 0, 0]`, log wealth `1.9917` against `0.1527` for the best single asset | `1e-12` | `1.8e-12` | `2.5e-12` | `4.3e-13` |
| 2 | interior | defaults | `4.9e-8` | `6.8e-8` | `2.3e-9` |

`T * objective_value(res.model)` equals the recomputed log wealth to `1e-11` at tight tolerances, so the model's objective is the prototype's. The residual at defaults is the solver's, not the formulation's: Clarabel's default `tol_gap_rel = 1e-8` puts the weights at `5e-8`. Two random fixtures with distinct drifts both land on a corner at `T = 60`, which is why the second fixture is constructed; the corner case is also the prototype's own correctness check (the best CRP is at least the best single asset), and both fixtures pass it.

The script is not committed; it lives at `tmp/bcrp_check_1150.jl` in the research worktree and is reproduced by the parameters above.
